# Britain and Wales — Timeline, Geography and Connective Tissue

Companion to `Britain (blog notes).md`. That file argues *why* to write Britain and proposes the
guidebook format; this one is the material.

Swept from `Scaunç.txt` (32,284 lines), `Data/pre-2022 notes.txt`, `borlish sentences with
translations.txt`, `all_claims_final.yaml`, the Obsidian vault, `Data/output_directory/` and
`World 2026 - Canon Survey.md`.

**§1–§3 invent nothing** and are traceable to a cited line. **§4–§6 are proposals** and are
labelled as such.

> **A note on sourcing.** A large share of the best Wales material is in `Scaunç.txt` **but not in
> `Data/output_directory/`** — the split script skipped 424 blocks that had no header, and several

## 1. Geography

### 1.1 The polity

**Britain** (Cambrick *Prydein*) is a polity of Albion centred on **Wales**, extending through
**Devon** and **Dunclothe**, out to **Kernow** and **parts of Ireland** (`Mapbook/Britain.md`).
A second canon gloss expands this: "a state comprising Wales, Devon, Kernow and parts of
**northern** Ireland and Albion" (`Scaunç:4999`).

The fullest real-world gloss in the corpus is the Wessern footnote at `notes/142.5`:

> "…Britain [**Wales, Devon and Cornwall, Brittany, Cumbria, Strathclyde, Man and the Isles,
> parts of Ireland**]"

So: Wales, the whole southwestern peninsula, Brittany, the northwest of England, the southern
Scottish lowlands, the Isle of Man and the Hebrides, and part of Ireland. Britain is by area
**the largest polity on Albion**, and the only one that is simultaneously Insular and Continental.

### 1.2 The tallaths

Provinces are **tallaths** (Borlish *taleð*; `borlish sentences:2451`, `notes/111.5`).

| Tallath | Entered | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Devon** [Devon + Cornwall] | c. 1280 | Conquered by Ivan of Cartaw; tallath declared by David IV (`notes/63.5`) |
| **Kernow** [Brittany] | C15 | "as it incorporated territories further afield" (`notes/135.5`) |
| **Dunclothe** [Cumbria + Strathclyde] | C15 | Suzerainty formalised at the founding (`notes/161.5`) |
| **Sodrick** [Man + the Hebrides] | C15 | "an outlying tallath… when it was formed" (`Mapbook/Sodrick.md`) |
| **parts of Ireland** | unstated | — |

Historic Welsh polities absorbed into the heartland: **Wynneth / Guineth** [Gwynedd], **Powes**
[Powys], **Guent** [Gwent], **Mondwell** [Anglesey] (`Scaunç:8180`, `notes/22553`, Canon Survey).

### 1.3 Physical geography attested

Thin, and worth knowing before you write an itinerary.

- **The Severn** — the historic frontier. The **Severn Line** was policed by standing levy forces
  on both sides c. 800 (`excerpt/82.1`). The **Great Border** was established between the mouths
  of the Severn and the Thames in **796 N** by the Merch subjugation of East Anglia, per the
  Oxonian 796 chronicle (`Scaunç:4889`).
- **The Monnow** (Welsh *Mynwy*), at Tremonnow, and the **Ux** [Usk], "from an old Welsh word for
  water" (`notes/142.5`).
- **The Clothe** [Clyde] valley, from which Dunclothe extends "down along the coast to the **Bay
  of Morcambe**" (`notes/161.5`).
- **Mondwell** [Anglesey], partitioned between Wynneth and Sodrick per the Ulstre and Carlile
  chronicles (`Scaunç:8180`).
- **The English Strait**, which divided the Insular Kelts "at some point before Caesar's first
  invasion"; later the **Irish Sea** — "although of course this neat partition is confused by the
  Scots and by the **Welsh communities along the Rustigh**" (`Scaunç:4889`).
- The **Rustigh Strait** itself [Dover–southern North Sea], which links Albion to Borland.

> **No Welsh mountains are named anywhere in the corpus.** No Eryri/Snowdonia, no named peak, no
> pass, no named Welsh harbour except Deganny. For a guidebook this is the largest single
> geographical blank, and it is yours to fill.

### 1.5 Languages

- **Cambrick** (Welsh) — the state language. Three living standards: **British**, **Angelican**
  (New Leudong) and **Hasinick/Hasinic** (Hasiny), all mutually intelligible but orthographically
  distinct (`notes/150.5`, Canon Survey:105).
- **Wessern** (Merch *Wessern*, Wessern *Wesseron*, Welsh *Sasneg*, Borlish *Wessaxon*) — the
  English minority language, "primarily in **eastern Devon and the downstream Severn**",
  **co-official in some tallaths** but "a decidedly subordinate role". Geminate consonants,
  simplified coda clusters (*darick* for Merch *dark*), fricative voicing (`notes/185.5`,
  `Scaunç:26855`).
- **Merch speakers in eastern Wales** are numerous enough that England's *linguistic* definition
  overlaps Britain's territory (`notes/185.5`).
- **Kernish** — implied by the "bilingual **Kernish–Wessern** version" of *Pasti Mezziterreni*
  published by Iscombe Editors in 1892 (`Scaunç:26593`).

## 2. The timeline

### 2.1 Prehistory and Rome (to c. 450)

- Ptolemy's **Pritannoi** denotes the Insular Kelts as a single group; the grouping is divided
  first by the English Strait before Caesar's first invasion, later by the Irish Sea (`Scaunç:4889`).
- Caesar's expeditions to Britannia, **55 aN** (`Mapbook/Borland.md`).
- **Claudius** orchestrates the conquests of Britain and Borland; **Hadrian** later adopts
  different strategies in each — "The Wall and the Hedge", per Bervisson's *An Eternal Rome:
  Ships of Theseus* (`notes/26.5`).
- "By the third century, Borland was more secure than Britain, having been conquered entirely,
  and thus required fewer [garrisons]" (`all_claims`).
- **Devon** is named for the *Dumnōniī* < Classical Kelt *duβn* "deep" (`notes/63.5`,
  `Scaunç:4480`).

### 2.2 The petty kingdoms (C5–C8)

- Germanic migration into Albion C5–C6 establishes Markland, Kent, Wessex, Anglia, Northumber
  (`notes/40.5`).
- Saxon encroachment drives **Kelt emigration from Devon to Kernow** — the founding movement of
  Brittany (`notes/40.5`, `notes/135.5`).
- **656** — Battle of **Gaulford**; the whole peninsula falls to Wessex, "although much of Cornwall
  had never entirely submitted to English rule" (`Scaunç:764`).
- **late C7** — **St David** dies 1 March at **Meneva** (later Tyðewi) (`Scaunç:2924`).
- **c. 700** — the Kernovan kingdoms unified under widowed **Queen Maud of Angeus**.
- **720** — Maud dies; **Vigo** succeeds. The **Catumagin Accord** fixes the Frankish border
  (`notes/135.5`).
- **c. 725** — Vigo marries **Winmar** (vault: *Guinnumar*), daughter of **David I of Wynneth** —
  the marriage that buys Welsh assistance (`notes/18.5`).
- **C8** — Vigo the Magnificent reconquers Devon; two decades of war end at **Petherton**; crowned
  first king of Greater Devon, *rex Magnæ Dumnoniæ* (`notes/63.5`).
- **before 800 N** — the **Brethin** founded at Tremonnow under Vigo's daughter **Iseld**. Named
  for their heavy cloak, Old Welsh *bretin* (`notes/142.5`, `notes/23.5`).
- **796 N** — Merch subjugation of East Anglia establishes the **Great Border** (`Scaunç:4889`).
- **c. 800** — **Owain of Brochwel** and Theodric the Awefast maintain levy forces on the **Severn
  Line** even in peacetime (`excerpt/82.1`).

### 2.3 The Viking Age (C9–C10)

- **824** — raids begin in Albion with the destruction of **Halgrive** (`notes/40.5`).
- **883 N** — **Alan III** crowned king of Greater Devon; loses Kernovan territory to the Franks;
  grants Devon to his nephew **Gorvan of Kernow** (also **Golleck**) (`all_claims`).
- **887 N** — the **Reconquest of Legaster** (`notes/3.5`).
- **888 N** — the monastery at **Landvennick** burned (`notes/135.5`).
- **Owain ap Blethyn**, legendarily **Owen of Jolliad**, "sometime-friend sometime-foe of the
  Arthurians" — evidence for Welsh-born participation in viking raids (`notes/3.5`).
- **C9** — Sodrick settled by Norse; **Harald the Blue of Sodrick** helps establish the Guithle
  kingdom of Scotland (`Mapbook/Sodrick.md`).
- **928** — Northumber takes **Eidin** from Dunclothe (`notes/161.5`).
- **c. 930** — **Wolfston the Younger**, last English king of Northumber, ends "ignominiously and
  without issue" at Edinbrough, per the **Carlile chronicle** (`Scaunç:26855`).
- **934, 942** — Dunclothe under **Roderick** takes Arran and Man from the Danes of Sodrick.
- **Wynneth consolidates primacy over Powes**, funded (the exam question suggests) by the Dane
  wayports between Dublin and Yorwick; **Mondwell partitioned** between Wynneth and Sodrick
  (`notes/3.5`).
- **1001–1026** — **Pope Agatho canonises David**, "commonly told" to be a signal of Papal support
  for "the **foundering Kingdom of Wales**, which had been losing people and land both to Markland
  and to Devon" (`Scaunç:2924`). Wales at the millennium is *weak*.

### 2.4 The Drengot century (C11–C12)

- **c. 1000** — Wales unified (`notes/40.5`).
- **c. 1029** — **Cadwallan**, erstwhile king of Wynneth, "finds himself the **unexpected** ruler
  of all Wales — a fortuitous position of strength against equally formidable Markland under
  **Oswin the Becoming**" (`Scaunç:4180`).
- **1028 / 1029** — the **Kernovan Conjuring**. Cadwallan keeps Wales "mostly free of Drengot
  control… by steering a course between Greater Devon and the Drengot, neither of whom wanted the
  other to have dominion over Wales" (`notes/116.5`).
- **C11** — Arthurian tales **first codified in Drengot England**, later "reworked substantially in
  post-Imperia Wales" (`Scaunç:6682`).
- **1120–21** — **Vigo VII** of Greater Devon invades the south coast of Wales, attempting
  reconquest of the southeastern principalities — **without** the support of Emperor **Roger II**,
  whose treasury was spent on the southern war against Willemy and who ruled from Paris through
  his brother Matthew in London. It fails (`Scaunç:13969`, `notes/223.5`-adjacent, `all_claims`).
- **early C12** — **Pope Nuntius** reforms the Julian calendar. See §2.6.
- **C12** — the **Brethin of Kernow** grow sharply in membership and are employed as advisors in
  courts across Europe (`all_claims`).

### 2.5 Welsh expansion (C13)

- **1199–1208** — **Pope Damian VI "the Wise"**, first Brethin ever elected to the Roman see, and
  **uncle of Catrin of Kernow and Alan VI of Greater Devon** (`excerpt/131.1`, `Roman Papacy.md`).
- **1225** — the **Realignment**: Alan VI takes Devon de facto independent of Kernow.
- **1232/33** — **Catrin of Kernow**, married into the Borlish nobility, **assassinated**. The 1292
  chronicle *Y Við a Catrin de Cornou* is **the first long-form work in Borlish (rather than Latin)
  about a layperson** (`excerpt/131.1`).
- **c. 1270 N** — "Even **Bristow** was wrested from Wessern authority for at least a decade"
  (`borlish sentences:387`).
- **c. 1280** — **Ivan of Cartaw** conquers Devon **without his liege's assent**; King David
  retroactively legitimises it, creating the Tallath of Devon for Ivan's son **Marduth**, Ivan
  having died of illness shortly after (`Scaunç:28341`).
- **1301** — **Andrew II of Markland** sacks Rexam / Car Vantel, reigniting the **Mallor Wars**;
  the original St Brigid's abbey burns (`notes/111.5`).

### 2.6 The calendar — the single largest connective fact in the corpus

From a 1961 Ruthenian *History of Timekeeping* (`Scaunç:2924–2935`):

> Pope Nuntius, early C12, "instituted a reform of the heretofore universal Julian calendar. Most
> relevant to us was **the shift of the New Year from the Annunciation on the 25th of March to
> David's Day, on the first**… It is thus unsurprising that **the Welsh New Year celebrations are
> a global legend**."

**Every "N" date in the entire corpus is dated from a Welsh saint's day.** The Nuntial calendar —
the setting's universal dating system, the reason 220.5 has to note that "the Nuntial calendar year
begins on March 1" — is Welsh. The Augsburg dominions rejected it as one of the Pauline
Denunciations and kept the Julian until the Rhine Compromises.

The same passage contains the other great find:

> "I myself was fortunate enough to be in **Deganny** for the **200th anniversary of the coronation
> of the People's King** — my hearing has never been the same!"

A **People's King** of Britain, crowned c. 1750s–60s given a 1961 author attending the bicentenary
— i.e. **contemporaneous with the Democratic Wars** and London's independence in 1748. And it
explains the otherwise unexplained dictionary sentence at `dictionary/101.3`: *"New Year's in
Deganny is a harrowing experience."* Deganny is the party city, and both festivals land there.

And a third: the passage is captioned

> "[Pictured left, the flag of the **modern Kingdom of Wynneth** (*Y Deyrnas Gwyneð*), using the
> colours of Saint David]"

**Wynneth exists as a kingdom in the modern day**, despite the Canon Survey filing it under
"absorbed / historical". See §6.

### 2.7 The Kingdom and the ocean (C15–C16)

- **mid-C15** — "the accelerating flow of goods from **Welsh Albion**" is one of the two pillars of
  Vascony's economic position under Ambrose III (`Scaunç:5044`). Wales is a major exporter
  *before* the Kingdom is founded.
- **before 1435** — **Margaret of Iscombe** [Exeter] marries Mark of Tolose. Their children are
  **Alexandra**, **Ambrose III of Vascony** and Victor of Astorga. **The Vascon royal house that
  made the Novomundane Landfall is half-Devoner.**
- **C15** — the **Kingdom of Britain** is founded.
- **Wars of Fealty** (late C15 – early C16, 46 years) — triggered by the Redstone Chair. Britain
  starts strong, extracts concessions.
- **Alexandra of Vascony's second marriage** "began (and had issue) during the Wars of Fealty but
  before her famed voyage" — and a film review explicitly uses the "palaces and castles of Huzatre
  and Cartaw" to "connect **the Welsh tangles in Albion** to the second marriage of Alexandra of
  Vascony and her famed voyage" (`Alexandra of Vascony.md`, `excerpt/70.1`). **Alexandra married
  into the British royal house.**
- **1483** — **Battle of Rexam**. Last **Duke of Chestre** killed; lasting control of Rexam; the
  follow-up attack on Chester rebuffed; Britain then withdraws from thirty more years of war.
- **early C16** — **King Howel's** resupply ports down the west African coast, built for the Asia
  run. British and Morrack mercantile domains "had overlapped in **Gaul and Spain** for nearly two
  centuries" (`Scaunç:6531`).
- **1516–17** — the **Phœbos** under **Martan mac Kellot** rounds Africa, calls at Ungotia, is
  directed on by Omani and Chinese traders via Gurjat and Madray, and reaches **Borune**. At
  Sinquan it meets the Morrack **Cynthia**. In the 1927 film *One Day in Borune*, mac Kellot's
  first mate shouts ***O Gastor 'ila Bolux!*** "From Castor to Pollux!" — al-Casmi's otherwise
  impeccable logbook is missing exactly those pages. mac Kellot declines to circumnavigate.
- **1518–25** — the **Second Great Dying**. Albion is exhausted; Britain is not.
- **C16** — the **Huzatre Psalter**: cheap prints with suggested folk tunes that "brought [the
  vintedos] into the voices of **every village congregation**" (`notes/230.5`). A Welsh press
  makes a Provincial verse form global.
- **1520** — the **Hangthen Edict**, issued in response to "the planned circumnavigation of the
  globe by Morrack and **British** ships" (`notes/179.5`).

### 2.8 The Novomund and the Democratic Wars (C17–C18)

- **C17** — staddomains along the Mendevan Gulf → **British Mendeva**, largest **Hasiny**.
- **1621** — the **Becouin Treaty**; broken after the **British Sack of Santrafew**, in which the
  Mastoisèt were "alleged to have collaborated" (`excerpt/111.1`).
- **mid-C18 Democratic Wars** — "many states including **China and Britain** quarrelled for
  [control over parts of India, including Madray]" (`all_claims`, `notes/81.5`).
- **c. 1750s–60s** — the **People's King** (§2.6).
- **after the Angelican Landfall** — emigration from Britain plus Cathayan state-building forms
  **New Leudong**.

### 2.9 The Workshop and the overtaking (C19)

- **1805 / 1810** — **killdark** [uranium] isolated in the Irish shire of **Killdara**; the word
  enters every language via **Welsh *kildárc*** "from Killdara" (`notes/126.5`).
- **mid-C19** — the **Glamour of Deganny** records the underworld slang giving Borlish **vlangr**
  (`dictionary/216.2`).
- **1870** — ***A Cyclopædia of British Persons***, 3rd ed., Wolfston Potter, Yievle Brethin
  Primers.
- **1880s** — a burst of Welsh-language publishing: ***Cludor ncar Vantel*** "A Postman in Wrexham"
  (1882), a time-loop novel that **gives the world the phrase *speid dolenu* "time loop"**, calqued
  into many languages (Borlish *liesc bouclant*); ***Dug y Gilfah Dowł*** "The Duke of Dark
  Corners" (1884) by **Taguen ah Owain**, an alt-history of the Medieval Imperia ruled from
  Damvath, Florence, Tanger and Kiew; ***Murro Mendeva*** (1888) by **In mac Harlach**, "an Irish
  Welshman", a polemic against integration with Mendevan peoples that "**reveals the influence of a
  resentful bloc in Britain which saw the rise of Hasiny as a threat**" (`Scaunç:26595–26603`).
- **1890** — ***Men of Jet and Diamond*** by Hasinick Welshman **Cadogan Torrior**, published by
  **Blackburn Editions**.
- **1892** — Iscombe Editors' bilingual **Kernish–Wessern** *Pasti Mezziterreni*.
- **late C19** — **British Mendeva overtakes Britain** in population and industry. Élite
  transmigration and intermarriage. Britain among the earliest embracers of the **Household
  Renovation**, alongside the Riverine Cities and New Leudong (`all_claims`).
- A Merch history concedes: "Our neighbours to the east have never matched the **globe-spanning
  workshop and household vanguard of the British**" (`notes/25.5`).

### 2.10 The modern era (C20–C21)

Almost everything here is cultural. See §3.

- **1903** — first radio built in **Hasiny**; **sobot** enters the languages.
- **1931** — *Children of the Battle of Rexam* by **Jon Paldreth**.
- **1934** — ***The Ascendant*** (*Hwn a Guodoð*), a **Welsh-language film** by director Dormaget
  on the years after the 1471 Landfall — with the Huzatre and Cartaw scenes.
- **mid-C20** — the **Tremonnow Open School** (formerly the Brethin Mesh Institute) teaching in
  Welsh and Wessern across Albion and British Mendeva.
- **1999** — *The Grimmer Hall* / ***Łis y Grimrageð*** "The Computing-Women's Hall", published in
  **New Leudong Cambrick**.
- **2005** — ***Y Ðreic o Redrwth*** by **Thoffina'h Girmont**: King Junderth's court at Redrouth,
  and Crown Prince **Arthur**, a duellist taverner whose mastery of words nobody has noticed.
- **1991–95** — the Culombálu steamship at Deganny.

## 3. Gaps in the timeline

Ordered by how badly a guidebook would feel them.

1. **The entire twentieth century.** No Millstone War role, no Drengot Collusion membership or
   refusal, no Good Game alliance, no City Wars, no LGT entry, no post-1961 position. Britain is
   the only major polity in the setting whose modern politics is a blank page.
2. **The founding of the Kingdom itself.** "The fifteenth century" is all canon says. No date, no
   founding monarch, no instrument. The single most important event in the polity's history is
   undated.
3. **The monarchy.** Named rulers of *Wales* are David I (C8), Cadwallan (C11), David IV (C13),
   Howel (C16) — and then nothing until "the People's King" (C18) and a modern Kingdom of Wynneth.
   No dynasty name, no regnal list, no capital of record.
4. **1301 → 1483.** A hundred and eighty years between the Sack of Rexam and the Battle of Rexam
   with nothing in it — no Black Death analogue in Wales, no revolt, no Mallor Wars campaigns.
   This is where the Kingdom gets founded, so the gap swallows §3.2.
5. **Ireland.** "Parts of Ireland" / "parts of northern Ireland". Never specified, never dated,
   never explained. Killdara is Irish and gives the world its word for uranium — but whether it is
   *in* Britain is unstated.
6. **The C17–C18 at home.** Everything attested is overseas (staddomains, India). Nothing about
   Britain itself between the Phœbos and the People's King.
7. **Physical geography.** No mountains, no named harbours except Deganny, no passes, no islands
   except Mondwell.
8. **Kernow's modern condition.** Canon Survey marks it ● "Tallath of Britain since the C15" — but
   a Brittany governed from Wales for six hundred years has no modern description at all.
9. **Where Huzatre is.** Fifteen mentions, no gloss.

## 4. Connective tissue — proposals

**Not canon.** Each of these closes a gap in §3 using only material already attested, and each is
chosen because it *explains* something the corpus already says rather than adding to it.

### 4.1 The founding: make the Kingdom a settlement, not a conquest

Canon says the Kingdom "formalised the suzerainty" Wales already held. The word is *formalising* —
an administrative act. **Proposal:** the Kingdom of Britain is proclaimed as a **compact of the
tallaths**, each retaining its own courts and levies, in exchange for a single crown and a single
fleet. That is the same constitutional form as Axbane and Pentrose inside Borland, and as Pojon and
Strengom on the Danaw — which the Canon Survey identifies as "this world's characteristic form".
It also explains why Kernow, Sodrick and Dunclothe all join *at once* and why they never leave.

### 4.2 The People's King: Britain's Democratic Wars settlement

Canon gives you a *People's King* crowned in the 1750s–60s and a Democratic Wars period in which
London becomes independent and Britain is fighting in India. **Proposal:** Britain's Democratic
Wars end not in a republic but in a **re-founded, deliberately popular monarchy** — the crown
detached from the old dynasty and re-granted by the tallaths' assembly. This gives you:

- a national holiday at Deganny that canon already requires,
- a reason Britain stays out of the Drengot Collusion a century later (a crown that derives its
  legitimacy from a compact of provinces cannot be pooled into an alliance-federacy),
- and a clean contrast with Borland, which abolished its monarchy in 1894 for the *opposite*
  reason.

### 4.3 The Collusion: an explicit, principled abstention

**Proposal:** Britain declines the Drengot Collusion and says so publicly. Its Atlantic interests
run west to Hasiny, not east to Roun; its constitution cannot delegate; and its nearest neighbour
London joined. Canon's silence becomes a stated policy. This also gives a modern reason for the
"resentful bloc in Britain which saw the rise of Hasiny as a threat" (`Murro Mendeva`, 1888) — the
bloc lost the argument, twice.

### 4.4 Wynneth: the crown's own title

**Proposal:** the modern **Kingdom of Wynneth** is not a rival state but **the personal title of
the British crown** — the way the heartland kingdom's name survives as the dynasty's own. Its flag
uses St David's colours because the crown's legitimacy runs through David's Day. This reconciles
the 1961 caption with the Canon Survey's "absorbed" list at zero cost, and explains why the
People's King is crowned at all rather than acclaimed.

### 4.5 Huzatre: put it in the north-west

**Proposal:** Huzatre is the **northern royal seat** — Cartaw south, Huzatre north — somewhere
around Caernarfon or Conwy, which puts it beside **Deganny** and beside **Mondwell**. That single
placement makes sense of everything attested about it: a castle-and-palace pair with Cartaw, a
sixteenth-century press (a cathedral or collegiate town), a modern city big enough for detective
fiction, and a neighbour to the New Year city.

### 4.6 The Severn Line as the spine of the itinerary

The Severn is attested as frontier in **796**, **c. 800**, **1121**, **c. 1270** and **1483**, and
it is still the Wessern language boundary today. **Proposal:** structure the guidebook's land route
along it — a border you cross rather than a territory you tour. Every century of the timeline has
an event on that line.

### 4.7 Fill the 1301–1483 gap with the fisheries

Canon gives Dunclothe's North Atlantic cod fisheries in the C14–C15, exporting via the Borlish and
Vascon meshes as far as the eastern Middlesea and acting as "a notable source of specie for the
vicinity" — and separately gives "the accelerating flow of goods from Welsh Albion" enriching
Vascony by the 1450s. **Proposal:** the C14–C15 is Wales's **maritime apprenticeship**. Dunclothe's
cod money is what pays for the fleet that later builds Howel's African ports. That makes the
Kingdom's C15 founding an *economic* event — the tallaths federate because the fisheries and the
Welsh export trade need one fleet and one flag — and it turns the Phœbos into the end of a
century-long story rather than a bolt from nowhere.
