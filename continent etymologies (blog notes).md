# Continent Names: Etymological Dossier

Working notes for the blog post on continental nomenclature. Canon is separated from gaps throughout; every canonical claim carries a source reference.

---

## The structural finding

The three groups are not parallel, and that is the most interesting thing about them.

- **Vetomund names are inherited, not coined.** Europe, Asia and Africa keep their real-world names unchanged. This is forced by the point of divergence: these names were fossilised in antiquity, long before Borland changes anything. The Boralverse cannot rename them without breaking its own premise.
- **Novomund names are coined, and we can date them.** Mendeva and Cappatia are both post-1471 inventions, and canon preserves the moment of coinage for one of them and a dating constraint for the other.
- **The third group is coined *and* retrofitted.** Angelica and Australia were reached by a southern and eastern route, not across the Atlantic — so they do not naturally belong to an Old/New binary defined by that ocean. Any collective term for them is necessarily a later, tidier-than-reality imposition.

That last point is the spine of the post: **the third Mund is a schoolroom category, not an explorers' one.** It explains why no name for it feels natural — because in-world, none would be.

### The buried joke, and the best thing in the post

Look at what the *-mund* series is actually made of. *Vetus*, *novus*, *sērus* — **old, new, late**. Every one is a temporal adjective. The Vetomundine scheme presents itself as geography and is in fact a chronology: the world we always had, the world we found, the world we got round to last. It sorts landmasses by when Europe noticed them.

Now put Cappatia beside them. Quechua *pacha* is not "world" in the European sense at all — it "encapsulates the physical expanse of the world alongside its temporal flow, forming a holistic construct that defies Western bifurcations of these concepts". *Kay pacha*, "this world, the present world", is one term in an indigenous **tripartite** scheme: *hanan pacha* above, *kay pacha* here, *ukhu pacha* below.

So: Vetomundine geographers assemble a three-world scheme sorted by time, and — without the least idea what they are doing — name one of its three members with a borrowed word that already meant "the present world" and already belonged to a three-world scheme sorted by time and space at once. They even picked the middle one.

The Vetomund got the past, the Novomund got the new, the Seromund got the latecomer — and Cappatia, alone among them, was already correctly named before anyone in Europe arrived to name it.

## Group 1 — The Vetomund

> *Vetomund*: "the Boralverse term for the Old World" (`all_claims_final.yaml`). Borlish *Vetomond*, adjective *vetomondin* — attested in the corpus, e.g. "recent magn demay por pudour vetomondin suffigr" ("far too recently for Vetomundine sensibilities").

| Name | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Europe** | Canon, unchanged | Used throughout. `Mapbook/Europe.md` defines it as "a continental peninsula in the northern hemisphere" with no etymological gloss. |
| **Asia** | Canon, unchanged | Used as a live geographical term — al-Casmi arrives somewhere "almost recognisable as Asia". Also productive: the archipelago named **Asia Cahal**, modern **Cahalla** (`Data/pre-2022 notes.txt`). |
| **Africa** | Canon, unchanged | `Mapbook/Libya.md`: "a Middlesea polity on the north coast of Africa". Note **Ifricia** as Libya's Single Caliphate–era name — the Arabic form is live in-world, giving you a doublet to play with. |

**The angle for the post:** the Vetomund names are the control group. They demonstrate that the Boralverse's naming divergence is strictly post-classical — nothing before Rome's collapse can move. Ifricia/Africa is the one place where the alternate history does touch a continental name, and only obliquely.

## Group 2 — The Novomund

> *Novomund*, also "the New World": "the lands to the west of the Atlantic Ocean… comprises the two great landmasses of Mendeva and Cappatia, along with several dozen major islands (such as Brasil and Cocain)" (`Mapbook/Novomund.md`). Borlish *Novomond*, adjective *novomondin*.

### Mendeva — fully canon, best-documented name in the setting

> "While we can confidently pinpoint the first usage of the name Mendeva (a surviving map from the Landfall voyage itself is marked *terrae mendevalensis*, after the Vask *mendeval* 'west')…" — `borlish sentences with translations.txt`

This is your model case: a vernacular descriptive adjective, Latinised on a working chart, hardened into a proper noun. Coined 1471, on the voyage itself.

### Cappatia — etymology settled, dating fixed by canon

**Run'allo [Quechua] *cappacha* < *kay pacha* "the middle world; the present world" → the relevant variety of Arabic → contemporary Latin → Cappatia.**

Canon independently fixes the date. In the study-notes excerpt covering al-Casmi's 1515–16 voyage, the narrator writes that the *Cynthia* "continued along the Cappatian coast", with the footnote:

> "(\*Remember that the continent would not be so named for several more decades yet.)" — `Data/pre-2022 notes.txt`

So **Cappatia is named c. 1540s–1570s**, several decades after Mendeva. Morrack ships had already circumnavigated "via Cappatia" by 1519, so the landmass was rounded well before it was named — and the Arabic stage in the chain is exactly what you would predict from that, since Morrack and the New World Company were the ones on that coast.

Three things worth drawing out:

- **The category error is the setting's house style.** *Kay pacha* does not name a landmass; it names a cosmological register, the world of the living and the present. Europeans took a term for *when and how you exist* and made it a label for a coastline. This is the same move as Brasil (myth → chart label), and the same move as the real-world *Eora* (a word meaning "here" taken for an ethnonym).
- **The sound-change vignette.** Quechua *-cha* /tʃa/ reaches Latin scribes who write it *-tia* — a sound-for-sound-sensible equation, since medieval Latin *-ti-* before a vowel was already assibilated. That single orthographic decision then feeds both English *Cappatian* and Borlish *cappaçan* /kapaˈtsan/, the ⟨ç⟩ faithfully reflecting a Latin *-ti-* that was never in the Quechua. The affricate survives in both daughters, in neither case as itself.
- **Endonym pattern.** *Run'allo* joins Yolŋu, Eora, Koori, Palawa and the rest — languages and peoples whose names mean, roughly, "people". Worth one line if you want a bridge from this post to the Angelica material.

### Supporting Novomundine names (all canon)

- **Brasil** — from the Irish myth of *Oil Breasail*, "a landmass west of Ireland that was said to be shrouded in mist except for one day every seven years". Canon explicitly contrasts it with Mendeva: "Unlike the name Mendeva, it is not known when the name Brasil was first used." Ghost-labels "appear sporadically on late-medieval maps of the Atlantic coast, usually much smaller and further east than the real place" (`Mapbook/Brasil.md`).
- **Ambrosia** — dynastic honorific, "named Ambrosia in honour of Ambrose III of Vascony" (`Mapbook/Ambrosia.md`). Vascon/Vask form *Ambroxa*.
- **New Provence** — transferred toponym, "named for Provence, a small Middlesea state".
- **Arcabil** — the Caribbean; no etymology in canon. Another open slot.
- **Cocain** — a major Novomundine island; no etymology in canon. The Cockaigne resonance is presumably deliberate and worth a line in the post.

**The three logics visible here:** descriptive-Latinised (Mendeva), legendary mislabel (Brasil), dynastic honorific (Ambrosia). Every Novomundine name fits one of the three.

## Group 3 — Angelica and Australia

### Angelica — newly canonised, no entry yet

- Australia in our terms; from *wäŋa-ŋaraka* in a relative of Yolŋu Matha, originally denoting the northern coast specifically, later generalised to the whole landmass once it was identified as one.
- **Caveat:** the gloss of *ŋaraka* is unverified. My recollection is "bone", which would make the compound something like "bone country" — an ancestral or burial ground, and an excellent reason for the term to be coast-specific before it was continental. Check a Djambarrpuyngu dictionary before committing this to print. Since the source is a *sister language* rather than Yolŋu Matha itself, you have licence to adjust the form.
- **The folk etymology writes itself:** *Angelica* will attract to the plant (*Angelica archangelica*) and the given name, and thence to angels. A continent whose name plausibly means "the place of bones" being popularly derived from angels is exactly the setting's register of irony.
- The demonym **Angelican** is now canon (25 occurrences), including in **the Angelican Landfall**. The near-homophony with *Anglican* is live and available.
- `Mapbook/` still has no `Angelica.md`; `[[Angelica]]` is an unresolved link.

### Australia (= Antarctica) — no canon whatsoever

Nothing in the repo. Entirely yours to build. The obvious and satisfying move is that *Terra Australis* attached to the actual southern continent because the northern one got a native name first — i.e. **Angelica's early naming is precisely what freed the name Australia**. That is a clean causal chain and the best single paragraph available to you in this whole post.

One collision to manage: **the Convoy Australier**, an eleventh-century chivalric order of the Duchy of Crain, officially the Order of Saint Hemma of Still Water (`all_claims_final.yaml`). *Austral-* is therefore already in use in a Carinthian sense, centuries before any southern continent is named. Either ignore it, or make a joke of it.

## The third Mund: **Seromund** — settled

> **Seromund**, from Latin *sērus* "late" — "the late world". Borlish ***Seromond***, adjective ***seromondin*** (patterning with attested *vetomondin*, *novomondin*).

Comprising **Angelica** and **Australia**. The name is prosodically exact against its siblings — *Ve-to-mund*, *No-vo-mund*, *Se-ro-mund*, all σ-o-mund — and semantically it names the real historical fact rather than an ordinal position: not "the third one", but *the one that arrived late to the scheme*. Which, given that the scheme is a retrofit built to accommodate it, is pleasingly circular.

It also completes the temporal series set out above: **old, new, late**, with Cappatia's *present* smuggled in from outside.

Nothing in the repo yet uses the term, so there is nothing to replace — it wants writing *in*, not swapping. Candidate homes: the terminology table in `CONTEXT.md`, a `Mapbook/Seromund.md` alongside `Novomund.md`, and the still-unwritten `Mapbook/Angelica.md`.

**One thread left dangling:** if Seromund is a late coinage, someone coined it, and the setting's habit is that such terms arrive contested and decades after the fact (the Romantic Wars went a century unnamed; *masquira* traces to a single 1860 novel). A losing rival or two in the scholarly record would cost you a sentence and buy a lot of texture. The rejected candidates below are available as exactly that.

### Why the other candidates failed

*Tertiomund* and *Tritomund* are ordinals: they say only "the third one we got round to", which is true but inert, and they invite the question "third by what ordering?". *Ultromund* and *Ultimomund* say "beyond" and "last", which are relative to a viewer standing in Europe — fine for a sixteenth-century chart, weak as a modern schoolroom term.

The prosodic target is tight: *Vetomund* and *Novomund* are both **σ-o-mund**, one syllable before the linking vowel. Matching that rhythm mattered more than the semantics.

### The also-rans — available as in-world rivals

| Coinage | Latin | Sense | Borlish | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Libramund** | *lībra* "balance, scales" | "the balancing world" | *Libramond*, *libramondin* | The strongest loser. Encodes the *reason* Europeans expected a southern mass at all — one required to balance the north. A learned coinage flattering the theorists who predicted it, and exactly the sort of term that survives in academic Borlish while losing everywhere else. |
| **Austromund** | *auster* "south wind" | "the southern world" | *Austromond* | The plainest, and therefore the most likely to win in ordinary speech. Collides with the Convoy Australier and with Australia — arguably a feature, since the Austromund *contains* Australia. |
| **Ceteromund** | *cēterus* "the rest" | "the world of the remainder" | *Ceteromond* | The etcetera-world. Wry, faintly administrative — the register of *staddomain* and *Œcumen Count*. |
| **Ignotomund** | *ignōtus* "unknown" | "the unknown world" | *Ignotomond* | Direct *Terra Australis Incognita* pedigree. A syllable too long for the pattern, and awkward once the place is well known. |
| **Imomund** | *īmus* "lowest" | "the nethermost world" | *Imomond* | Matches the rhythm exactly and sounds genuinely old. Risks reading as an underworld. |
| **Contramund** | *contrā* | "the world opposite" | *Contramond* | Clean antipodean logic; slightly combative. |
| **Notomund** | Gk *nótos* "south wind" | "the south world" | *Notomond* | Crisp, but a near-minimal pair with *Novomund*. Probably fatal. |

**Avoid Meridiomund**: *Meridian* is already taken in-world as a place-name — al-Casmi puts in at "the various Arcabil and Meridian outposts".

A further option, if you want a colloquial layer beneath the Latin: a Borlish native compound in the transparent Germanic style of *steeplepost*, *killdark* and *voidtale* — a *Mond Souðer* or similar — as the form people actually say.

## Open slots, ranked by usefulness

1. **Australia/Antarctica** — nothing at all in canon. Highest payoff, no constraints to honour. The causal chain to write: Angelica's early native naming is precisely what left *Terra Australis* free to attach to the real southern continent.
2. **Arcabil** and **Cocain** — referenced frequently, never explained. Arcabil is the more conspicuous gap.
3. **Seromund's coinage moment** — who, when, and what it beat.
4. **`Mapbook/Angelica.md`** and **`Mapbook/Seromund.md`** — both unwritten; `[[Angelica]]` is currently an unresolved link.
5. **Verify *ŋaraka*** before the post goes out.
