# The World in 2026 — Canon Survey

A compilation of everything currently canon about the modern political map of the Boralverse.
Compiled from the Obsidian vault, `Data/output_directory/` (notes, excerpts, dictionary), `Scaunç.txt`,
`borlish sentences with translations.txt`, `all_claims_final.yaml`, `CONTEXT.md` — and
`Data/pre-2022 notes.txt`, whose numbered footnotes gloss real-world equivalences that later drafts drop.
That last file is an archive of superseded drafts, but it is **not** redundant: Caddisow and Bamackon are
defined nowhere else.

**This document does not invent anything.** Where a modern status is not stated in the sources, it says so.
Inference is confined to the bracketed *reading* notes and to the closing sections.

---

## How to read the status marks

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **●** | Attested as existing in the twentieth or twenty-first century (or spoken of in the present tense) |
| **◐** | Exists in canon, but the latest positive attestation is pre-modern; present-day status not stated |
| **○** | Name only — listed or mentioned in passing, with no substantive description |

Real-world equivalences in `[square brackets]` are the project's own glosses, taken verbatim from the sources.
A `~` inside them is the project's own "approximately".

**The present frame is 2026.** The latest in-world documents in the corpus are the *Alster Almanac* (Markland
town council, Spring 2026) and Łew ab Ennis's mesh series *Pa's Hyd Raf?* (2026); a braxoch-beet note is dated
"as of 2025"; *The Structure of Twentieth-Century Polities* is 2024; *Follow Suit* is 2023.

## 1. The global frame

### Continents

From the *Concise Onomatory o'Merch*, 6th ed., 2001 — geographers have used a three-realm division since the
early nineteenth century:

- **Vetomund** — Africa, Asia, Europe and their off-lying islands. [Old World / Afroeurasia]
- **Novomund** — **Mendeva** [North America] and **Cappatia** [South America], plus the **Arcabil** [Caribbean] and other islands.
- **Ceteromund** — **Angelica** [Australia] and **Australia** [Antarctica], plus **Tecamaw** [Aotearoa NZ] and similar landmasses.

Note the false friend: in-world **Australia is Antarctica**; Australia-the-continent is **Angelica**.

The Pacific is the **Lovac Ocean** (adjectivally *Lovac*, as in the Lovac Isles and the Lovac Wars).

### The ideological axis

The defining twentieth-century division is **Ubiquity** vs **Vicinity** (per Ollamec Vamncoury,
*The Light that Casts a Thousand Shadows*, 2017):

- **Ubiquity** — equality, universalism, homogeneity. Nineteenth-century Mashick was a hotbed of its early form; post-Millstone Mashick pursued a "one world language". Mashick underwent an "Ubiquitous revolution" in the 1930s.
- **Vicinity** — liberty, localism, diversity. Linked to regional-language revival and to nontraditional family structures; also to ghettoisation and ethnostates. Its intellectual pedigree is traced back to the Provincial Secession from Burgundy and to Jaume fi Gustau's advocacy for Doccian.

Critically, **this is not a two-bloc world**: *"It is nonsensical to say there is a united Ubiquity bloc."*
Vamncoury's whole thesis is that the two frameworks are not cleanly opposed and that the same policy has been
justified by both. Any 2026 map should therefore avoid a NATO/Warsaw-Pact colouring.

### Political vocabulary

- **collujon / collusion** — bloc, alliance-federacy; originally pejorative, now neutral.
- **alliance-federacy** — the characteristic supranational form (Drengot Collusion, Riverine Cities, Danaw Concert, New Urban Mesh, Stadbund).
- **staddomain** — a polity grown out of a colonial trading post; inhabitants are *staddenzen*.
- **tallath** — a province of Britain (Devon, Dunclothe, Sodrick).
- **double-crown** — a political form referenced in the LGT charter discussion ("half of a double-crown?"); never defined. **Gap.**
- **paramontað** — suzerainty/liegedom, the inverse of vassalage.
- **copperplate / tinplate / anthracite** — political faction labels, coined by David Bensaíd in *The Three Cities* (1751) from the factions at the court of Malik Hisham Ar-Rashid of Libya (r. 1601–10). Darchild's Collusion government was **tinplate**; Borland's 1981 government was **anthracite**.
- **ostracon reforms** — the Kentish post-1961 constitutional model, subsequently copied "in several polities around the globe".

## 2. Europe

### 2.1 The Northwestern Isles — Albion, Ireland, Borland

#### Borland ● — *Istr Boral*
The core setting. A **single polity** occupying the whole island. Capital **Damvath**, on the Dam.

- **Republic since 1894.** Queen Natalia II abdicated on Revillon 1894 amid the upheaval of Borland's accession to the Drengot Collusion; the monarchy was dissolved with her.
- Collusion member 1894–1961. Several Borlish cities (Axbane, Pentrose — never legally independent) had already joined via the New Urban Mesh.
- **1981: an anthracite administration**, with a Ministry for Schools issuing the *Report towards a Normal Teaching for Children at Mantelschool*.
- Modern machinery of state (from *Follow Suit*, 2023): a **director** [president], a **Charter** constraining fiscal action, an agency issuing fiscal reports, **Ministries** including **Land** (*Feu*) and **Family** (*Famigl*), **Councillors** (*Scavin*), an **Axbane office**, and contested **elections**.
- **Axbane and Pentrose retain independent courts and taxation powers** — explicitly compared to Pojon and Strengom on the Danaw.
- Regions: **Angland** (west; regional capital Vithor), **Southbar** (southwest; Axbane), **Çadrosc**, **Lascoum**.
- Cities: Damvath, Axbane (*Ausbagn*), Vithor, Cordin, Pentrose, Neuvort, Combert, Dambourne, Haldanvion, Noccair.
- Universities/institutions: New Vithor University, Noccair Lyceum, Jacobin University of Axbane, University of Cordin, Borrhatic School of History (Combert), Palaç Jagovin (the parliament building, late C18).

#### Britain ● — Cambrick *Prydein*
Kingdom centred on **Wales**, founded in the fifteenth century by formalising Welsh suzerainty over its tallaths.

- Tallaths: **Devon**, **Dunclothe**, **Sodrick** [Man and the Hebrides], plus **Kernow** [Brittany] and **parts of Ireland**.
- Founded British Mendeva (Hasiny etc.) from C17 staddomains along the Mendevan Gulf.
- Retained cultural prestige over British Mendeva into the C20 even after being overtaken in population and industry in the late C19; heavy élite intermarriage and transmigration across the Atlantic.
- **British Welsh** is a distinct standard from **Angelican Welsh** and from **Hasinick Welsh** — three living Welsh standards, so all three polities persist.

#### Markland ●
The most densely attested modern polity after Borland. East of Wales, north of London and Kent, south of Northumber and Dunclothe.

- Capital **Tommarth** [Tamworth]. Second city **Utter Safford**, 4.4 m at the **2010 Œcumen Count** (a regular census).
- Extent in 2000 is mapped (`- Images/Markland 2000.png`): roughly the English Midlands plus East Anglia and up into southern Yorkshire/Lancashire — **not** the West Country, the South East, Wales, or the far North.
- **Alster** [Alcester] is a commuter town fifteen leagues (30 mi) south of Tommarth. Its Spring 2026 town-council listings show: the **Ides of Breakfast** [Eid al-Fitr] as a public town celebration; the **Riverbank Masket**, the *second-oldest continuously-operating* mosque in Markland; a lineball club in the Second Level; Easter services; access by **slate** [smartphone] and **quister** [phone].
- Other centres: Conster [Colchester] (a New Urban Mesh city), Rotcham [Rochdale], Safford [Salford], Lester [Leicester], Makerfell, Lincon, Nottingham, Rexam.
- Spellings were rationalised during the Long Peace (Colncester → Conster).
- Institutions: Royal Tommarth Castinghouse (publisher, *Excourse Puzzlers* 2021), Markland Ministry for Health, Conster Health Edifice, Conster Open School.

> **Note.** A well-established, long-normalised Muslim population in the English Midlands is one of the sharpest
> single differences from our own map, and it dovetails with the Islamic conquests of Kiew and Cazany (§2.6).

#### Kent ● — formally *the Bond of Kent and Normandy*
A cross-Channel state: southern Albion **plus** northern Gaul.

- Roun French is its administrative language; **Roun** [Rouen] holds the government buildings, including the **Red House** (the Ministry for Information — the Collusion's intelligence arm, and the fuse of the Furore of 1961).
- Political centre of the Drengot Collusion alongside London and Paris.
- **The Kentish ostracon reforms** followed the Collusion's collapse and became a global model.
- Won the LGT once, in **1965** (captained by Vincent Margouille).
- Kentish is the only English language with a uvular rhotic ("the roral ambit").

#### London ●
Independent **city-state** since 1748 (Democratic Wars), under Queen Margaret. The polity comprises much of the Tames watershed; borders Markland (N), Kent (S), Britain (W).

- The **Lord Mayor** rivals the monarch in power.
- Home of the **New Lyceum** (late C18 research university) and of **Hatfell**, a northern town near the Markland border.
- Led the New Urban Mesh into the Drengot Collusion c. 1890.
- Enduring association with **spycraft** — the 1887 Desarden Affair (its ambassador to Nackon Day), and Londoners in the Roun Red House at the Furore of 1961.

#### Northumber ◐
East-coast polity bordering Markland, Dunclothe and Scotland; largest cities **Yorwick** (capital) and **Edinbrough**.
Listed among the polities of Albion, but the narrative stops at the C11–C12 as northernmost Drengot vassal.
**Modern status unstated — a significant gap.**

#### Scotland ◐
Guile kingdom established in the C9 with the help of Harald the Blue of Sodrick, displacing/merging with Pict
culture. Described medievally as "the two-minded Kingdom of Scotland". **Orkney and Shetland** were "the last
remains of Horther rule in Albion" as of the high medieval period. **Modern status unstated.**

#### Ireland ◐
Britain holds "parts of Ireland". Historical polities named: Connaught, Lenster, Munster, Tyrconnal, Dublin
(a Dane kingdom in the C10–C11 confederation). Post-1961 the Hatfell Honours cover "all of Gaul and the
Northwestern Isles [Ireland, GB, Borland]", so Ireland is inside the modern cultural sphere.
**Its internal political division in 2026 is unstated — a significant gap.**

#### Absorbed / historical on Albion
**Wessex** (subdued by Kent by c. 1000), **Anglia** (annexed by Markland, Treaty of Oxford, late C8),
**Essex**, **Alba**, **Gwyneth**, **Guineth**, **Powes**, **Mondwell** [Anglesey], **Markish Lincon**, **Chestre** (last Duke killed 1483).

### 2.4 Italy and the two Papacies

The single most consequential fact here is that **there are two Popes**, and both are still elected:

> "the archbishop of **Waduz** [Vaduz, Liechtenstein] … is the only Christian official who can vote as a cardinal
> in the conclaves for electing **both the Augsburgher and the Roman Popes**." — *Everywhere and Everything*, 2000

- The **German Secession** (C13, from the Pauline Denunciations in Bavarn) created the **Augsburg See** as a rival to Rome and collapsed the German Empire.
- The **Great Fracture** (mid-C16) gutted Roman temporal power. On paper the Pope remains an absolute ruler of scattered Italian and German provinces; in practice **the Triunvirato** governs the city of Rome.

#### The Danaw settlement — the clearest modern arrangement in the corpus

From *Downstream: Afterlives of the Riverine Cities* (Zorica Branimirova, University of Belgrade, 2019):

1. The **Kingdom of the Danaw** ruled the basin for two centuries; its zenith was the **Decadence of Belgrade**, when it took **Bulgary** from the Second Roman Empire after that empire's C17 collapse and pushed back the Bavarn border. It **did not survive the Second German War (1815–23)**.
2. In its place rose the **Riverine Cities** — a treaty federacy of city-states along the river: **Belgrade, Boxa** [Novi Sad], **Gollamo** [Ruse], **Pojon**, **Strengom**. A hotbed of religious minorities, Promethean clubs, the Deviance movement, and (by the 1810s) a semi-public **tovaric** subculture — the word *tov* is first attested here. Belgrade hosted the 1850 **Laic Declarations**; the Cities were among the earliest adopters of the Household Renovation; Boxa opened the first regularly ticketed **house of odyssey** [cinema] in 1912.
3. The **Millstone War's** reorganisation took the upriver cities: **Pojon → Moravy**, **Strengom → Awry**, by 1920. Both nonetheless **keep independent courts and taxation powers** to this day.
4. The **Sava Compact of 1932** created the **Danaw Concert** ● downriver — the modern successor, retaining more of the Riverine character.
5. **Boxa declined full membership.** Its **1935 association protocol** leaves it neither fully sovereign nor fully subordinate — explicitly "the bane of cartographers the world over".

> **For the map:** Boxa needs a hatched or annotated treatment. This is the one place canon *tells you* the
> border is ambiguous.

### 2.7 Greece, Anatolia and the fall of the Second Roman Empire

- The **Second Roman Empire** (also *Empire of the Hellenes*, *Malchassian Rome*, *the Empire in Byzantium*) persisted over a millennium from Justinian's reconquest and **finally collapsed in the seventeenth century**, in some tragedy inside **Saint Sophia's** at Stamboul.
- In the 1820s Stamboul is "the recovering shell of Stamboul". **What replaced the Empire is never stated. Major gap.**
- **Principality of Mystra** ◐ [the Peloponnese] — a Crusader-era state administered by the Convoy Australier.
- **Mysarnos** [~Pylos] — a Revitalist hub city; the *Mysarnos Codex*.
- The **Hellenic Conflict in Armenia** of the 1640s was the latest of a series of Hellenic wars in the east for access to the Caspian.

**Sub-Saharan Africa holds exactly three named entities** — Caddisow, Bamackon and Ungotia — and the first two

else is unnamed: the Sahara, the Guinea coast, the Congo, East Africa between Ungotia and Caddisow, the Cape,
Madagascar. The structural hook is planted and unused: *"Borlish trade guilds were involved in the establishment
of staddomains along Atlantic Cappatia and Africa. Initially little more than trading posts, these would grow into
significant polities over the next centuries."* Those African staddomain-descended polities are **never named**.
This remains the largest gap in the setting.

## 8. The blanks — where canon runs out

Ranked roughly by how much map they leave empty.

1. **Sub-Saharan Africa.** Three named entities in the whole subcontinent — **Caddisow**, **Bamackon** and **Ungotia** — plus Sant Adrian and the Hadramot's Ethiopian reach. The Sahara, the Guinea coast, the Congo, the Cape and Madagascar are all unnamed, as are the Atlantic-African staddomains the sources say "would grow into significant polities".
2. **The successor to the Second Roman Empire.** It fell in the C17; Stamboul was a shell in the 1820s; nothing fills Anatolia, the Aegean or the Levant in the modern day.
3. **The Riyalid Sultanate's modern fate**, and with it modern Egypt, Arabia, Mesopotamia and the Levant.
4. **Japan (Nivon), Korea, Manchuria** — Nivon is a name and an etymology, nothing more.
5. **Russia and Central Asia.** Only Kiew, Cazany, Adigh, Tartary and Holmgrod are named, and the Islamic-conquest premise is stated without any resulting political geography.
6. **India.** Gurjat, Madray, the Jamishid Empire, Bengal and Seldiva are named; the rest of the subcontinent, and all of it after the C18, is blank.
7. **Angelica outside New Leudong** — no other polity named on the continent.
8. **Interior and Pacific-slope Mendeva** between Zangathy, Mivock, Ax and Mashick.
9. **Oceania** beyond Tecamaw, Bidia, Rhalodony and Havechine.
10. **Northumber, Scotland and Ireland in the modern day** — all three named as polities/regions but with no post-medieval political statement.
11. **Modern Italy** — how the Roman Papacy, the Triunvirato, the Augsburg See, Lombardy, Venise, Jeans, Tuscany, Naples and Sicily relate in 2026.
12. **The City Wars (1940s)** — one sentence exists. Given that they burned Anvers and coincide with "the outbreak of war" that delayed Japetos III, this is the setting's WWII-shaped hole.
13. **The Lovac Wars' full extent** — Mendevan, but who fought whom is unstated.
14. **The "double-crown"** — a political form referenced once and never defined.
15. **"Which modern polity claims descent from the Cammojan Kingdom?"** — still literally unanswered in the corpus, though **Tacday Ancore** (~Cambodia, and *Ancore* ~ Angkor) is the obvious candidate and is now recorded as such.

## 10. Reading of the shape of the 2026 world

Not canon — this is the pattern the canon suggests, offered as a starting point for filling blanks.

- **There is no superpower duopoly.** The one explicit statement about blocs is a denial of one. The great alliance-federacy of the C20 (the Drengot Collusion) *collapsed* in 1961 and left a de-concentrated northwest Europe — Kent, London, Borland, France, Burgundy, Willemy all separately sovereign, bound by shared honours, shared law-models and a shared week.
- **Cities punch above their weight.** London, Boxa, Axbane, Pentrose, Pojon, Strengom and the Stadbund cities all hold fiscal or judicial autonomy inside or across larger polities. The alliance-federacy and the semi-autonomous city are this world's characteristic forms — not the nation-state.
- **The Atlantic is the world's spine.** Morrack–Ambrosia, Vascony–Ambrosia, Britain–Hasiny, Provence–New Provence, Willemy–Neuwillem: each Vetomundane power has a Novomundane descendant that has since **overtaken** it. The Mendevan Unmooring inverted the old hierarchy, and the C19 élite intermarriage rebuilt it sideways.
- **The Islamic world reaches much further north and much further into the Novomund than ours.** Morrack made first Novomundane contact with Mashick and Tavancy; Arabic mediated most Novomundane placenames into European languages; Kiew and Cazany were conquered; a mosque in a Markland market town is second-oldest of many.
- **Christendom is permanently split three ways** — Rome (temporally hollowed), Augsburg, and the Grecian churches — with no Reformation-shaped realignment. That is why the Laic Declarations mattered so much.
- **The largest polities by area are Novomundane** (Mashick, Zangathy, Tavancy), and the most populous single states are probably Cathay, China and Mashick — but the corpus never ranks them.
