The Vetomund comprises the continents of [[Africa]], [[Asia]] and [[Europe]], together with their off-lying islands. Since the early nineteenth century geographers have divided the large landmasses of the world into three realms: the Vetomund, the [[Novomund]] and the [[Ceteromund]].

## Name
The name is found on world maps from 1689, and in print from 1692. It comes from Scholastic Latin *vetomundus* "old world", formed by analogy with *novomundus*, the name of the Novomund. The names of the three continents themselves descend directly from Latin.

## Africa
- [[Morrack]], in the north-west of the continent
- [[Libya]], on the [[Middlesea]] coast between Morrack and Egypt
- [[Egypt]], whose city of Cairo was the seat of the [[Single Caliphate]]
- Hadramot, a loose collection of wealthy city states and admiralties in Yemen and Ethiopia, controlling trade through the Gulf of Aden
- [[Ungotia]], a trading port of long standing
- Caddisow, a state in the south of the continent, opened in the mid-nineteenth century
- Bamackon, a major population centre of fourteenth-century western Africa
- Sant Adrian, an island under Vascon control since the fifteenth century
