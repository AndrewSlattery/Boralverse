The Atlantic Ocean (Borlish **occeyan Atlantic**) is the ocean between the [[Vetomund]] to the east and the [[Novomund]] to the west. The Novomund, or New World, takes in the lands west of the Atlantic, and [[Mendeva]] lies between the Atlantic and the Lovac Ocean.

## Geography
The [[Middlesea]] joins the Atlantic in the west, and the [[Graven Sea]] is an arm of the Atlantic in northern Europe, east of Denmark. [[Gaul]] has coasts on both the Atlantic and the Middlesea. In the west of the ocean lie [[Brasil]], an island in the North Atlantic off the eastern coast of Mendeva, and the islands of the [[Arcabil]], east of [[Mashick]].

## History
### Fisheries
In the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, North Atlantic fisheries led by [[Dunclothe]] were a major exporter of dried and cured cod, which they sold through the meshes of [[Borland]] and [[Vascony]] as far as the eastern Middlesea. British dominion over the Atlantic had long frustrated the Vask fishing families. To break it, [[Ambrose III of Vascony|Ambrose III]] founded the [[New Navarre Enterprise]] in 1458, to secure Vascon supremacy in the trade of North Atlantic fish through the Middlesea and to create trade ports down the western coast of [[Africa]]. Its ships explored the northern Atlantic further than anyone since the Danes.

### The crossing
Islands labelled Brasil or similar names appear sporadically on late-medieval maps of the Atlantic coast, usually much smaller and further east than the real place. In 1471 a ship of a New Navarre Enterprise fishing expedition in the North Atlantic sighted and landed on Brasil, the [[Novomundane Landfall]]. The *Estellas daus Sanz*, which would have been the second lone ship to make the Atlantic crossing, sank in infamous circumstances.

In the following decades many Novomundane crops, among them tashban and shockle, crossed the Atlantic from Mashick. By the end of the sixteenth century the trade network of [[Provence]] reached across the ocean to port towns in Mendeva such as the newly founded [[Santrafew]]. In the latter half of the century German expeditions explored Atlantic [[Cappatia]], and trading operations from [[Willemy]] founded staddomains there, notably Neuwillem. In the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries Borlish trade guilds helped found staddomains along Atlantic Cappatia and Africa. In 1679 the Bordew-Audaz canal joined two rivers of Gaul, connecting the Atlantic to the Middlesea without passing through Morrack-controlled seas.

### Modern links
In the nineteenth century, faster ships and much shorter travel times brought the élites of [[Britain]] and [[British Mendeva]] together, with many of the new rich crossing the Atlantic and marrying across it. Even so, despite the spreading steeplemesh, scientific collaboration across the ocean remained slow, and several discoveries were made separately in the Novomund and the Vetomund. By the 1930s communication across the ocean was fast enough for papers such as *[[Fiellas Dimenja]]* to be published simultaneously in Santrafew and in Vascony.

In 1950 the Japetos III undersea barric steeplepost line, some 1,500 leagues long, linked the port of Gadire in [[Morrack]] with Paratzon in Brasil. It is the third cable to bear the name of Iapetos, the Titan who, as the father of Atlas, has given a ready name to successive attempts to span the Atlantic by wire. The Morrack and [[Ambrosia|Ambrosian]] governments conceived the project jointly in 1943; after disputes over funding and the outbreak of war, the line sent its first messages on 11 October 1950. In his later years the chef Shomoji Taxon presented the first cooking programme shown simultaneously on both sides of the Atlantic.
