Gaul (Borlish **Gal**) is a region of western [[Europe]], lying south of [[Albion]] across the Kentish Channel. It has coasts on both the [[Atlantic]] and the [[Middlesea]], and its north-western peninsula is [[Kernow]]. Its principal polities have included [[France]], [[Burgundy]], [[Provence]] and Norman [[Kent]].

## Antiquity
The ancient Gauls spoke Gaulish, a Kelt language. Several of its words survive in Borlish via Latin, among them *camin* "way", *carrug* "cart" and *boc* "mouth". The Gauls founded the city later known as [[Roun]] (Roman *Rotomagus*). Provence takes its name from the *Provincia Romana*, the first Roman province on the Gaulish side of the Alps. In the fourth-century Tetrarchy of Diocletian, Gaul and Roman Brittania together made up the District of Constantius.

## The Franks
Provence was incorporated into the expanding Francian Empire in the sixth century. Around 720 the Catumagin Accords ended hostilities between the [[Kingdom of the Franks]] and the Kernish kingdoms and fixed a border between them, part of which ran along the river Vire.

In the ninth century the Francian Empire divided in two. Its eastern half, combined with [[Willemy]], became the [[German Empire]], while the western fragment continued as the Kingdom of the Franks, or the West Francian Kingdom. In the mid-tenth century the West Francian king Robert I held court in [[Paris]], while the Kingdom of Aquitain was ruled from Bordew.

Viking raids along the Seine and the Orne began in earnest in the ninth century and then spread west; the monastery at Landvennick in Kernow was burned in 888. [[Normandy]] grew from Dane settlements in the north of Gaul in the ninth and tenth centuries, and the Franks formally ceded it in the tenth century. By the eleventh century it was the Duchy of Normandy, a semi-autonomous Frankish vassal ruled from Roun by the Drengot family.

## The Drengot Empires
In the [[Kernovan Conjuring]] of 1029, Normandy conquered Kent with the support of [[Devon|Greater Devon]]. Roger of Normandy went on to annex much of the rest of Gaul, founding the [[First Drengot Empire]], which at its height claimed control over much of Albion and most of Gaul. France and Burgundy were among its major vassals, along with the march (later duchy) of [[Tolose]] in the south. Provence was the last territory the empire conquered.

Burgundy ceased to be a Drengot vassal after the [[Emergency at Dijon]] in 1170, taking Provence with it, and the empire was formally dissolved in 1191.

## Late medieval and early modern Gaul
In the fifteenth century, during the [[Vascon Ascendancy]], [[Vascony]] conquered Tolose and took Burgundy's Middlesea coast, including Provence. Burgundy won Provence back in 1494, but the region broke away in the [[War of Provincial Independence]] of 1497–99, aided by King Marc V of France. In the first decade of the sixteenth century [[Barcelon]] took Tolose from Vascony, which, together with Burgundy's gains, left Vascony with no Middlesea coastline. France meanwhile grew hostile to Kent, then ruled by the Two Henries, for nearly two decades before the two reached reconciliation in the early 1490s.

After 1589 King [[David of France]] began to centralise power in France at the expense of his ducal and comital vassals. In 1679 the Bordew-Audaz canal joined two rivers of Gaul, connecting the Atlantic to the Middlesea without passing through Morrack-controlled seas. After the [[Blue Trans-Willem War]] of the late seventeenth century the Kingdom of Burgundy was dissolved, its territory partitioned between Provence, France and [[Bavarn]].

## Modern Gaul
The [[Modest Arrangement]] saw a resurgence in Gaul and [[Spain]] in the early nineteenth century, which has been blamed in part on the [[Deviance movement]]. Other accounts credit Deviance with contributing to the Arrangement's decline over the first half of that century. France was seen to have profited unjustly from the [[Second German War]], gaining significant territory without contributing much manpower or matériel.

In 1852 Norman Kent, France and Burgundy (re-formed without a monarch) united politically, and this nascent alliance became the [[Drengot Collusion]]. The [[New Urban Mesh]] of cities, including [[London]], joined it, and at its height the Collusion was the principal power of north-western Europe. It collapsed in 1961. Since then the Hatfell Honours, prizes for the arts first awarded across the Collusion, have been awarded across all of Gaul and the [[Northwestern Isles]].

## Polities and regions
- [[France]], a kingdom south of Kent
- [[Kent]], formally the Bond of Kent and Normandy, a state spanning southern Albion and northern Gaul, with its capital at [[Roun]]
- [[Burgundy]], historically ruled from Geneva, with its principal city at [[Dijon]]
- [[Provence]], on the Middlesea coast, first independent in 1497, with its capital at [[Marsella]]
- [[Vascony]], occupying parts of southern Gaul and northern Spain
- [[Kernow]], the north-western peninsula, now a tallath of [[Britain]], with the city of [[Nauned]]
- [[Tolose]], a city and region in the south, belonging to Barcelon since the early sixteenth century (barring some brief exceptions)
- [[Willemy]], east of Kent and France, with its subregion [[Avosche]]

## Languages
Varieties of French spoken in Gaul include Paris French, Burgund French and the Norman French of Roun. Provence has its own language, [[Doccian]]; Kernish is spoken in Kernow, Vascon in Vascony and Willem Dutch in Willemy.
