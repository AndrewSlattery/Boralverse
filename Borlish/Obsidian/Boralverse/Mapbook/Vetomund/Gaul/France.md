France (Borlish **Franç**) is a kingdom in [[Gaul]], lying south of [[Kent]], north of [[Burgundy]] and west of [[Willemy]].

## History
### The Franks
In the ninth century the Francian Empire divided in two. Its western fragment, the [[Kingdom of the Franks]] or West Francian Kingdom, was ruled in the mid-tenth century by Robert I (crowned 923), who held court in [[Paris]]; its other major towns included Reims, Strasbourg, Loyons, Genevre, Roun and Amiens. In the tenth century the Franks formally ceded the fiefdom of Dane settlement that became [[Normandy]].

### Under the Drengot Empire
France was one of the major vassal kingdoms of the [[First Drengot Empire]], formed in the eleventh century from the union of Normandy, France, Burgundy, Kent, [[Devon|Greater Devon]], [[Markland]] and York. During the eleventh century the emperors moved their court from [[Roun]] to Paris, and Emperor Roger II spent most of his reign there, leaving his younger brother Matthew to rule in his name in [[London]]. By 1120 much of the imperial treasury had gone on defending the long eastern border with Willemy, where a new Great Castle at Vésonçon, completed in 1119, was the last point in a defensive line from Flanders to the Alps. The empire was formally dissolved in 1191.

In later centuries the [[Brethin]] order spread from the Briton states into France.

### The fifteenth and sixteenth centuries
As the [[Wars of Fealty]] engulfed [[Albion]], France spent nearly two decades in growing animosity towards Kent under the Two Henries, but the two reached reconciliation in the early 1490s, and France stayed out of the wars. In 1497, on the advice of the general [[Guillen daus Sanz]], King [[Marc V of France|Marc V]] backed Lord Daniel Menton of the house of Amigòt when he and other nobles of [[Provence]] declared independence from Burgundy.

From the early sixteenth century France held the duchy of [[Avosche]] in Willemy, which remained a stable French vassal for over a century. Its duchess [[Adaillé Nassow, Duchess d'Avosche|Adaillé Nassow]] (1524–1608) had great influence at the court of King [[David of France|David]], and rumours persisted that she was in fact his mother. After a scandal of 1589, when her grandson and heir fled across the Atlantic to [[New Provence]], David gained more political independence and began to centralise power in France at the expense of his ducal and comital vassals.

### The seventeenth to nineteenth centuries
Avosche changed hands five times during the seventeenth century, most bloodily in the [[Blue Trans-Willem War]]. After that war the Kingdom of Burgundy was dissolved and its territory partitioned between Provence, France and [[Bavarn]].

France was seen to have profited unjustly from the [[Second German War]], gaining significant territory without contributing much manpower or matériel. In 1828 the Avosche city of [[Axton]] lay in French Guillenne, and a letter written that year calls Paris a "city of greed and carrion". France embraced the [[Modest Arrangement]] in the nineteenth century, even as the physician Mickel Cráyenshot publicly supported the [[Deviance movement]].

### The Drengot Collusion
In 1852 Norman Kent, France and Burgundy united politically. This nascent alliance became the [[Drengot Collusion]], which London led the [[New Urban Mesh]] to join around 1890. Alongside London and Kent, Paris was one of the Collusion's political centres until its collapse in 1961.

## Paris
Paris has been a seat of power since at least the tenth century, when the West Francian kings held court there, and the Drengot emperors ruled from it in the eleventh and twelfth centuries. Paris French is one of the northern French languages, the *lengas d'oil*, along with the Roun French of Kent. Paris University Editions is a publishing house.

## People
- Robert I, king of the West Franks, crowned 923
- [[Marc V of France|Marc V]] (fl. 1497), who backed the independence of Provence
- [[David of France|David]] (fl. 1590), who centralised royal power
- [[Rachel Desselut]] (fl. 1743), philosopher, author of *[[The Parable of the Mute Bookbinder]]*
- Mickel Cráyenshot (fl. nineteenth century), physician and early supporter of the Deviance movement
- [[Honorine Maignard]] (fl. 1890–1940), author and veteran of the [[Millstone War]]
- Agathe Bisset (died 2025), born in Blois, who held positions in the parliaments of France after the Collusion's dissolution and wrote *[[Men in Black]]*
