Flanders (Borlish **Flandr**; adjective *flamesc*, Flemish) is a region on the northern coast of [[Gaul]], across the [[Rustigh Strait]] from the south coast of [[Borland]]. It lies between [[Normandy]] to the west and [[Willemy]] to the east, and in the eighteenth century it formed part of Norman [[Kent]].

## History
### Medieval Flanders
In the eleventh century the Duchy of Normandy stretched from the Vire in the west to Flanders in the east. The Order of Saint James was founded in tenth-century Flanders under the patronage of Amelia of Bruges (Borlish *Amegl de Brug*). Around 1067 the order was implicated in a popular uprising in Flanders against the Drengot emperor Geoffrey II. The rebels were believed to have the backing of local nobility, but the emperor's supporters in Normandy mustered enough force for the rebellion to be quickly disavowed. The order fled to [[Axbane]] in Borland, where it built the Peninsular Monastery and is known today as the [[Jacobin Fraternity]].

The First Drengot Empire's defences against Willemy ran from Flanders to the Alps. By 1120 Emperor Roger II had spent much of the imperial treasury on this line of castles, completed with the Great Castle at Vésonçon in 1119. In 1121 he was in Paris, facing a depleted treasury and a "Flemish impasse".

### Norman Kent
In the eighteenth century "Norman Kent", the continental part of the polity of Kent, meant Normandy and Flanders. During the [[Democratic Wars]] the philosopher [[Rachel Desselut]] went into exile there.

## Culture
Flemish cloth was traded abroad, and Flemish performers toured; one letter-writer, newly arrived south of the Danaw, finds the local Dutch almost as awful as that of some Flemish performers seen in past summers. A now-moribund Flanders dialect may explain the spelling of the Borlish word *tuyaut* "pipe, cigar".
