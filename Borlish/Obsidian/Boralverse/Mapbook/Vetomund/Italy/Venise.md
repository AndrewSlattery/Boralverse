Venise (Borlish **Venis**) is a republic in [[Italy]], south of [[Furlain]]. It is one of the venerable mercantile republics of the [[Middlesea]], alongside [[Jeans]].

## History
### The Second Tetrarchy
Together with Jeans, the oldest of the so-called Latin Republics, Venise is a counterpoint to the notion that the [[Second Tetrarchy]] of the eleventh and twelfth centuries consisted solely of great centralised empires. In 1112 Venise took Ancona and its holding on Corfu, reaching a momentary territorial maximum. Around 1120 it was probably led by Parre Branco Magnossone, who held power from about 1120 to 1125 and again from 1136 to 1142.

### Rivals and trade
After the [[First German Empire|German Empire]] collapsed in the thirteenth century, Venise became Furlain's most frequent rival. The history of medicine *[[In Sickness and in Health]]* discusses the Venisian and Borlish trade routes alongside the Silk Road to Cathay and China. Ablate (negative) numbers were already recording debts in the account-books of Venise and other trade cities before European mathematicians made them respectable in the late fifteenth century.

Piracy was long the scourge of the Middlesea republics, Venise among them, and it thrived after the [[Novomundane Landfall]] of 1471. The Concert, the legislature of independent [[Provence]] after 1497, was modelled on that of the Venisians.

### Later history
In the eighteenth century, popular unrest in Venise and Crovatia over bread and grain stood as a warning to neighbouring [[Bavarn]], where famine threatened to destabilise the state.

In the nineteenth century the Venisian composer [[Chiarina Bruno]] wrote the opera *[[Wings Afire|Ale in Fiamme]]* (*Wings Afire*), first performed in the Verona Arena in 1824. During the [[Good Game]] period, Venise Italian borrowed *rasgatto* "resolution of a mystery" from the title of Olexi Harchenko's mystery trevold, and the word went on to many European languages. In the same period Campottonì in [[Mendeva]] was briefly known as the "Venise of Mendeva".

## Language
The language of Venise is [[Italian|Venise Italian]], also called Venisian.
