Lombardy is a polity in the north of [[Italy]]. It borders [[Furlain]] to the east and also borders [[Tuscany]]. Historically a kingdom, ruled in the twelfth century from Pavia, it is home to the Autonome University of [[Saint Marinus]], one of the oldest still-running places of learning in the world.

## History
### The Lombard kingdoms
Medieval Italy had more than one Lombard kingdom. From the tenth century into the [[Second Tetrarchy]], the [[Kingdom of the Lombards]] (and later Lombardy) contested Verona and its province with the [[First German Empire|German Empire]]. In 987 Lombardy conquered the Kingdom of the North Lombards, whose capital was Pavia. The North Lombard ruling family fled to the [[Second Roman Empire]], where some of them administered the empire's western themes. The accounts written by others among these exiles are history's strongest sources for certain events of the [[German Secession]].

### The Adelchians
In the eleventh century Lombardy was ruled by the Verona branch of the Adelchian dynasty, and the Autonome University was founded at Saint Marinus. In 1120 King Jacob III (1087–1130), the last of the Verona branch to grow up in Verona, ruled the kingdom from Pavia. A mapbook of the period describes Italy's north at this time as consolidating its power and looking south for new lands to conquer, with Rome, "the seat of all Christendom", as the greatest prize.

By the twelfth century the dattilo lines had become characteristic of processional chant in the churches of Lombardy and [[Burgundy]]. In 1216 scholars of the Autonome University compiled a *liber numerorum*, which contains the earliest-known enumeration of arrumb values in Europe.

### The fifteenth to eighteenth centuries
In the fifteenth century [[Vascony]] conquered enough of [[Provence]] to share a border with Lombardy and [[Jeans]]. In the sixteenth century the Mendevan émigré [[Maria Joquinnera]] spent time at the courts of Burgundy, [[Barcelon]] and Lombardy. During the [[Long Peace]] of the late eighteenth century, Lombard heredical theorists made notable advances in farming and husbandry.

### Modern Lombardy
In the 1820s, after the [[Second German War]], emigrants from Barcelon and Lombardy came in large numbers to [[Santrafew]] in New Provence. The Lombard marquisates, among them Cremuna, were abolished in the 1860s. During the [[Good Game]] period the Lombard diplomat Audace Saviano was instrumental in forming the Italian Concert in 1891. By 1970 Lombardy was importing [[Astraphor|astraphor]] from as far away as [[Portingale]].

## Language
Lombard is the language of Lombardy. Borlish borrowed *zizania* "discord" from it in the seventeenth century, and *bonaventur* "happenstance" via French from Lombard *bunaventura* "good fortune". Merch *ablate* "negative" comes from Lombard *ablad*, first used in financial accounts. Borlish etymologies also cite Lombard Dutch, a German language (for example *skirmen* "protect, shield").

## Places
- Pavia, former capital of the Kingdom of the North Lombards and the royal seat in 1120
- [[Saint Marinus]], home of the Autonome University and its Sovereign Library
- Cremuna, seat of a marquisate abolished in the 1860s

## People
- Jacob III (1087–1130), king of Lombardy
- [[Audace Saviano]] (fl. 1891), diplomat of the Good Game period, whose granddaughter was the author [[Honorine Maignard]]
- Jovanna Ariperto (fl. 1998), historian at the Autonome University and author of *Developments in Philosophy since the Revitalist Period*
- Dijaca Rofotto (fl. 1997), historian of medieval religion at the Sovereign Library, author of *On the Christianisation of the Northwestern Isles*
