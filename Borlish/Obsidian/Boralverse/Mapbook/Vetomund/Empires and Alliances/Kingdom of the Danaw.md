The Kingdom of the Danaw (Borlish **Reyaum Danavan** or *Reyaum ag Danau*) was a European kingdom that united the basin of the Danaw river politically during the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, ruling it for some two centuries. It collapsed in spectacular fashion after the [[Second German War]] (1815–23), and the [[Riverine Cities]] rose from its ashes.

## History
### Expansion
As the region was united under the kingdom, farama dry-pottage, a buckwheat dish that originated in Vallack communities, spread west upriver. It is still an important tradition from Pojon to Gollamo.

The kingdom's final decades before the Second German War, contemporary with the [[Long Peace]], are known as the [[Decadence of Belgrade]]. In this period the kingdom reached its height in territory, external political power and foreign relations, even as its internal economy and stability declined. It wrested [[Bulgary]] from the suzerainty of the [[Second Roman Empire]] after that empire's final collapse in the seventeenth century, and more recently consolidated its control over lands on its northwestern border with [[Bavarn]]. In 1816 Lengrod was a frontier city of the kingdom, contested with Poland.

### Religion and dissent
The kingdom had significant religious minorities, and the Church, of whichever stripe, was strongly associated with local authority. Albion shared these conditions, and in the late eighteenth century much of the development of Anti-Paradise theology took place in the Promethean clubs of Albion and the Danaw, precursors of the [[Deviance movement]]. The term *defiance*, from which *Deviance* may derive through a mistranslation from Wessern, is better evidenced in the Danawan clubs than in those of Albion.

### Collapse
The kingdom did not survive the Second German War. Its disintegration in the war's aftermath is the setting of Petcov's 1830s fright tale *The Red Man*, which features a newly independent [[Boxa]]. The disruption of the war and the collapse also meant that the observations of the sun that Stiláge Anna-Dorota made during an overshadowing in 1816, published the following year with her collaborators in Sobjan, went largely unread for a decade.

In the kingdom's place rose the Riverine Cities, a federacy of city-states along the river bound by treaty, trade and a pointed distrust of distant rulers claiming authority over the whole basin. Belgrade hosted the committee behind the [[Laic Declarations]] of 1850, whose passing has largely been credited to the strong influence of Anti-Paradise theology in the Cities after the kingdom's collapse.

## Language
The Borlish dictionary illustrates *contrdontaçon* "downfall" with the kingdom's fall: *Cay descreuçon dy contrdontaçon dy Reyaum ag Danau*.
