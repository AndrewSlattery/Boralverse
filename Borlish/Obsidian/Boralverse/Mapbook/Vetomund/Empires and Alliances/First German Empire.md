The First German Empire (Borlish **Empir German**), usually simply the German Empire, was a medieval empire of central [[Europe]]. During the [[Second Tetrarchy]] of the eleventh and twelfth centuries it was one of the four empires that between them held much of Europe, alongside the [[First Drengot Empire]], the [[Second Roman Empire]] and the [[Single Caliphate]]. It was often ruled from Bardwick. It disintegrated in the thirteenth century, and is distinct from the Second German Empire of the sixteenth century.

## History
### Origins and expansion
The German Empire descended from the eastern half of the [[Kingdom of the Franks]] after the kingdom split in the ninth century, combining the Eastern Franks with [[Willemy]]. In its first centuries it expanded east and south. In 1091, under Bertholdt II, Bohemia and Lower Pannonia were consolidated into the empire, a date sometimes taken as the start of the Second Tetrarchy.

### The dual imperium
The kingdoms of [[Saxony]] and [[Bavarn]] became the empire's preeminent vassals, and their dynasties, the Houghbrocken of Saxony and the Andex of Bavarn, ruled the empire in turn and for some time jointly. In 1120 the empire was ruled jointly by King Brunow III from Bardwick and King Conrad II from Regensburg. Its seat had not yet been fixed, and that year it was administered from Frankford. Tensions between the two kingdoms ran high, but the empire was then quite possibly the most centralised polity in Europe and Near Asia.

The empire included more than a dozen states. Besides Saxony and Bavarn, these included:
- the Duchy of [[Willemy]], a minor polity that formed much of the border with the Drengot Empire. Defence against the marksmen of Willemy led Emperor Roger II to fortify a line of castles from Flanders to the Alps, completed with the Great Castle at Vésonçon in 1119.
- the Kingdom of [[Bohemia]]
- the March of [[Furlain]], its southernmost part, at first a direct imperial vassal and later under the suzerainty of Bavarn
- the Duchy of [[Crain]], subordinate to Bavarn, which controlled the city of Trieste. The [[Convoy Australier]] began there in the eleventh century.

From the tenth century into the Second Tetrarchy, the empire contested Verona and its province with the [[Kingdom of the Lombards]], and later with [[Lombardy]].

### Decline
The northern cities began to drift away from imperial authority early. Their mayors were collaborating independently of their lieges even at the height of the Second Tetrarchy, and toll agreements in the 1120s between Lumbridge, Bardwick and Bremen are among the earliest contracts of what became the mercantile [[Stadbund]]. Hamburgh formally renounced the empire's authority in 1207, Dorstad in 1208 and Lumbridge in 1214.

The empire's slow erosion accelerated sharply with the [[German Secession]]. In 1225 Paul I/XI's [[Pauline Denunciations]] established a rival papal seat at Augsburg, with Bavarn as the epicentre of the schism. Over the following decades each duchy, and in some cases each county, declared either for [[Rome]] or for Augsburg. The resulting patchwork of allegiances brought the empire down "in spectacular fashion". Its collapse made [[Rome]] treat the [[Baptism of the North]] as urgent.

By the end of the thirteenth century "the fiction of a surviving German Empire was laid to rest with the corpse of the first Kingdom of Saxony", and the Houghbrocken were reduced to their core lands upstream at Lepzi and Magdeburg.

### Legacy
After the collapse, Furlain's most frequent rival became the Republic of [[Venise]]. By the sixteenth century a Second German Empire had arisen: Lumbridge was once again an imperial vassal, though Emperor Matthias II (r. 1542–1568) won only symbolic statements of loyalty from its council.

## Rulers
- Bertholdt II (fl. 1091), who consolidated Bohemia and Lower Pannonia into the empire
- Brunow III of Saxony and Conrad II of Bavarn, joint rulers in 1120
