Asia is one of the three continents of the [[Vetomund]], east of [[Europe]] and [[Africa]].

## Name
The name comes from Greek *Asíā* "Anatole", likely from Hittite *Assua* "north-west Anatole".

## Near Asia
- [[Persia|The Persias]], always plural in later usage
- Armenia, site of the Hellenic Conflict of the 1640s
- The Riyalid Sultanate, in the seventeenth century the largest Muslim state in the world, stretching from Iscandar to Medina and the Baghdad Exclave
- The Ibadine Imamate, seated at Moscat, which fell to the Hadramot in 1652

## India
- The Jamishid Empire, originally Turkic, ruling from Tammombay
- [[Gurjat]], whose seventeenth-century trade empire stretched from Ungotia and Sant Adrian in the west to [[Cathay]] and eventually [[New Leudong]] in the east
- [[Madray]]
- Seldiva, whose court received Danish astronomers in 1677
- Bengal, where the card game bonoro originated

In the [[Democratic Wars]] of the mid-eighteenth century many states quarrelled for control over parts of India. Both nearby powers like China and distant trade interests like [[Britain]] became embroiled.

## East Asia
- [[Cathay]], in the north, permanently sundered from China in the late fourteenth century. Under Kitan rule it deliberately set itself apart from its southern neighbour.
- China, also called the Southern Empire. In 1520 its Hangthen Edict declared that if the world could be girdled, China should tie the ropes herself.
- Nivon

## South-East Asia
- [[Nackon Day]], a kingdom
- Zampanagar
- Tacday Ancore
- [[Borune]], whose city of Sinquan holds an annual monsoon fair with a Chinese quarter, and site of the [[Great Borunesk Meeting]] of 1517
- Cahalla, an archipelago named *Asia Cahal* by the crew of the Morrack ship *Cynthia*

## Sea routes
Several European powers sought to reach Asia by sea. [[Britain]] under King [[Howel of Britain|Howel]] used resupply ports down the west African coast, and its ship the [[Voyage of the Phœbos|Phœbos]] reached Borune in 1516. The Morrack ship *Cynthia*, sailing west by way of [[Cappatia]], met it at the Great Borunesk Meeting the following year, so Morrack ships were the first to circumnavigate the world.
