China (Borlish **Scin**), also called the Southern Empire, is one of the two great polities of eastern [[Asia]], south of [[Cathay]]. Its ruler is the Southern Emperor. The two have formerly been united on several occasions, but they have been separate since the late fourteenth century.

## Name
The name China is likely a dynastic name, carried by sea around the coast of India to Sanscrit *Cīna* and Persian *Chīn*. The names China and Cathay were attached to what were believed to be separate realms long before the final sundering.

## History
### The Southern Emperors
Through the thirteenth century Cathay, under the Kitan Leu dynasty, conquered the divided south piecemeal. The conquest was at first successful, but it is thought to have caused enough resentment that when the Leu soon collapsed, the south quickly consolidated under the Southern Emperors.

### The age of exploration
News of western exploration reached the Southern Empire in 1468, when Chinese merchants at Mombasa met the crew of the [[Morrack]] ship *Orion*; their reports to the Southern Emperor are said to have changed the course of history. Nearly fifty years later, Chinese traders guided the British ship *[[Voyage of the Phœbos|Phœbos]]* on to [[Gurjat]], [[Madray]] and [[Borune]], whose port city of Sinquan held an annual fair with a Chinese quarter older than the sultan's conversion to Islam.

In 1520, hearing that Morrack and [[Britain]] planned to sail round the globe, and loth to be outdone, the Dinghoi Emperor (r. 1511–1534) issued the Hangthen Edict. It declared that if the world could be girdled, China should tie the ropes herself. In 1528 a treasure ship became the first Chinese vessel to alight in the [[Novomund]], on the Lovac coast of [[Mendeva]]. The voyages of this era are the subject of Quoming's *The Sea Peoples*, an epic of exploration considered one of the greatest Chinese works ever written.

### Later history
Several outbreaks of disease in China in the century before 1735 are now suspected to have been pennackit dysentery, which began its global spread in 1753. In the [[Democratic Wars]] of the mid-eighteenth century, China was embroiled in the contest for India, and it launched attacks on the eastern Indian polities through one of its protectorates. By the 1820s the European voidtale craze had spread as far as China.

## Language
China has several Chinese languages; etymologies cite a southern, imperial Chinese, and a local language of the coast. Borlish *cocheyau* "stilt" comes, via seventeenth-century travel writing about an acrobatic act in coastal China, from a local word for "stilts" meaning literally "high-tiptoe".
