Nackon Day (Borlish **Nacon Day**) is a kingdom in [[Asia]]. Its monarchy survived the [[Democratic Wars]], and in 1887 its king was caught up in the Desarden affair, a scandal of the [[Good Game]] period.

## History
Like the kings of [[Madray]] and many other royals who kept their thrones in the aftermath of the Democratic Wars of the mid-eighteenth century, the kings of Nackon Day saw their political powers significantly trammelled.

### The Desarden affair
In 1887 [[James Desarden]], the ambassador of [[London]] to Nackon Day, became the subject of a major scandal involving the King's eldest son. Desarden had a career in London's City Administration both before his assignment to Nackon Day and after the scandal. The affair is widely thought to have been politically motivated and a product of direct meddling from the [[New Urban Mesh]]. Its popular image, shaped by books and film, as a tale of glamorous espionage and political machination behind closed doors is not entirely deserved, and the reality was tawdrier. Despite the sleuthing of many amateur detectives, no conclusive link has been found between the affair and the appearance of Cráyenshot's great-nephew in the London melee. The affair helped cement London's enduring association with spycraft, and it is the subject of the 1968 film *[[Mr Desarden, Prince Consort]]*.

### Modern Nackon Day
Nackon Day has a Ministry for the Future, one of the several dozen Ministries for Consequences established worldwide, which monitor the effects of government policy.
