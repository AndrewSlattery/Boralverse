Cathay (Borlish **Cathay**) is a polity in the east of [[Asia]], north of [[China]]. The two have at times been united, but they have been separate for several centuries; the final, permanent sundering came in the late fourteenth century, at the end of an imperial dynasty.

## History
Under Kitan rule, Cathay is held to have deliberately set itself apart from its southern neighbours. One historical-linguistics thesis argues that this is why the soundwise use of Sinic runes, an old practice for foreign names and words, gained currency in Cathay at that time.

In the seventeenth century the trade empire of [[Gurjat]] reached as far east as Cathay. After the [[Angelican Landfall]], Cathayan state-building, alongside emigration from [[Britain]], formed the polity of [[New Leudong]]. It is named for the Cathayan peninsula of Leudong and was christened by a Cathayan captain. New Leudong has seen significant Cathayan nation-building since the late seventeenth century. The writer [[Pether Hetsong]] was of Cathayan and British heritage.

## Culture
- The Cathayan *tapsue* "magpie" tales are an old tradition that some scholars see as a precursor of the [[Masquira romance|masquira]] and kevler stories of nineteenth-century Europe.
- The Cathayan "surveyor's span" is one of the two lengths whose midpoint fixed the dime foot; the other is the Roman foot.
- Cathay, like China and the Persias, has had institutions monitoring the effects of government policy for centuries, arguably millennia.
