Ireland (Borlish **Irðon**) is the westernmost of the large [[Northwestern Isles]], lying west of [[Albion]] across the Irish Sea. Parts of it now form a tallath of the Kingdom of [[Britain]], the most populous of Britain's outlying tallaths.

## History
### Early Ireland
Ireland's Iron Age began, as on the other Northwestern Isles, around the ninth century aN. Its people were among the Insular Kelts, whom Ptolemy appears to have described as a single group; that grouping was later divided by the English Strait and then by the Irish Sea. The Roman Empire, which conquered all of [[Borland]] and part of Albion, never attempted Ireland.

Ireland's early monastic communities were comparatively insular, unlike the outward-looking monasteries of Borland. The [[Brethin]] order, founded in the eighth century, was originally devoted to chronicling the Christianisation of Albion and Ireland.

### The Danes
The first Viking raids struck the shores of Albion and Ireland, and Danes settled in Ireland during the Norrish ascendancy of the eighth and ninth centuries. Dublin became a Dane kingdom, and Dane traffic between Dublin and Yorwick may have funded the rise of Wynneth in [[Wales]]. As the [[Dane Supremacy]] waned, Dublin joined a confederation of local Dane kingdoms in which [[Northumber]] was first among equals. When [[Dunclothe]] took control of [[Sodrick]], some of Sodrick's people fled or were expelled to Ireland.

### The four kingdoms
A map of Albion and Ireland in 1028 shows "the ever-fractious kingdoms of Ireland" recently reduced to four by the breaking of the Ultagh in the north-east: Tyrconnal, Connaght, Lenster and Munster. Tyrconnal and Lenster fought wars against the Norrish to the east and the other Irish to the west at the same time, while the houses of Connaght and Munster vied for the position of High King of Ireland.

### Ireland and Britain
In the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries the fish and salt trade gave the nobility of the eastern Irish coast, along with those of Wales, Dunclothe, Sodrick, Kernow and Devon, the habit of thinking of themselves as having common political interests. Parts of Ireland became an outlying tallath of the Kingdom of Britain.

In 1824 the assayer and alchemist Yorreth ap Trahaen isolated an unknown mineral from the mines of the Irish shire of Killdara. A Dublin pottery maker at first monopolised it for yellow-green glazes. The substance, killdark, is named via Welsh *kildárc* "from Killdara", and it is now the corporal used in the vast majority of threshold mills.

## Language
Irish is a Kelt language, related to Welsh and to the old Kelt language of Borland. Etymologies compare Irish *mac* "son", *frágh* "heather" and *márah* "tomorrow" with Welsh and Borland Kelt words. The Irish myth of Oil Breasail, a misty land west of Ireland, almost certainly inspired the name of [[Brasil]].
