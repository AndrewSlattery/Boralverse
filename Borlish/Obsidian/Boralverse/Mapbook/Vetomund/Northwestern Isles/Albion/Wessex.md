Wessex (Borlish **Vessax**, *Reyaum Vessaxon*) was an English kingdom of [[Albion]], founded by the Saxons in the west of the island in the early medieval period. At its height it ruled [[Devon]] and held [[Kent]] as a vassal. Most of its former territory now lies in the English part of Kent, north of the [[Kentish Channel]], and its westernmost lands are part of [[Britain]]. Its language, [[Wessern]], is now spoken mainly by the English minority of Britain.

## History
### Foundation and expansion
After the Romans left, Germanic migrants from the continent established several kingdoms in Albion through the fifth and sixth centuries. The Angles founded East England and [[Northumber]] in the east, and the Saxons founded Wessex and [[Markland]] in the west. Saxon encroachment westward drove Kelts from what would become Devon across the sea to [[Kernow]]. After the final defeat of the West Albic Britons at the [[Battle of Gaulford (656)|Battle of Gaulford]] in 656, the whole Devon peninsula was under the sole authority of Wessex, though its remotest parts are thought never to have fully submitted. Kent was a vassal of Wessex during the kingdom's rise and fall, gaining power and some autonomy as Wessern might diminished.

### Loss of Devon
In the first half of the eighth century [[Vigo the Magnificent]] of Kernow, with Welsh help, began a reconquest of Devon. After more than two decades of intermittent warfare with Wessex, his victory at Petherton in 749 made him the first king of Greater Devon.

### Decline
During the [[Dane Supremacy]] the collapse of Wessex was one of the upheavals that let Dane war leaders seize territory. A map of Borland in 900 still shows Wessex across the sea to the west, alongside Northumber and Markland. By the turn of the second millennium, however, an expanded Kent had subdued the remains of Wessex. Under the long-reigning [[Rodulf of Kent|Rodulf]], Kent held a recognised supremacy over the rump Wessex, though Greater Devon contested it.

After the [[Kernovan Conjuring]] of 1029, Wessex was one of the kingdoms of the [[First Drengot Empire]], alongside West Francia, Markland and Dumnonia. Wessern authority survived into the thirteenth century, when it lost Bristow on the Severn estuary for a decade or more around 1270 to the expanding kingdom of [[Wales]].

### Today
Wessex is no longer a polity. Most of its lands belong to English Kent, the part of Kent north of the Kentish Channel, while its westernmost parts lie in Britain.

## Language
Wessern (Borlish *Wessaxon*, Welsh *Sasneg*) is one of the four English languages of Albion, along with Northumber, Kentish and [[Merch]]; of the four, only Northumber has fewer speakers. It is now spoken mainly in eastern Devon and along the lower Severn, and is co-official in some of Britain's tallaths, though in a decidedly subordinate role. It is marked by geminate consonants, simplified coda clusters and voiced fricatives between vowels and at the start of words, as in *darick* "dark" (Merch *dark*).
