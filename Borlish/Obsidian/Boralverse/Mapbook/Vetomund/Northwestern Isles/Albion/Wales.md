Wales (Borlish **Cambry**) is a land in the west of [[Albion]] and the heartland of the Kingdom of [[Britain]]. Britain's outlying tallaths lie around it: [[Ireland]] to the west, [[Devon]] and [[Kernow]] to the south, and [[Dunclothe]] and [[Sodrick]] to the north. Its long-standing rival [[Markland]] lies to the east, across the Severn.

## History
### Early kingdoms
Roman rule reached only part of Albion. It left the Britons of the north and west a few roads, several hundred words of Latin, and a lasting distrust of their south-easterly neighbours.

In the early medieval period Wales was divided among several kingdoms and principalities, among them Wynneth, Powes and Guent. Around 725, [[Vigo the Magnificent|Vigo]] of Kernow married Winmar, daughter of King David I of Wynneth, and this marriage probably won him Welsh help in his reconquest of [[Devon]]. While forming Greater Devon he also took the lands around [[Tremonnow]] from Guent.

Raids during the [[Dane Supremacy]] mostly struck the British periphery, but some Welshmen went raiding in their turn. In the romances set in this period, Welsh vikings are stock characters, while their Welsh victims rarely appear at all. Wynneth emerged from the Supremacy as the strongest of the Welsh kingdoms, perhaps funded by Dane traffic between Dublin and Yorwick. It absorbed Powes, split Mondwell with Sodrick, and by the year 1000 was the core of a united Wales.

### Saint David
David, a Welsh bishop, died at Meneva on the first of March towards the end of the seventh century. He was canonised during the pontificate of Pope Agatho (1001–1026), possibly as a sign of Papal support for the young Welsh kingdom, which was then losing land to Markland and Greater Devon. When Pope Nuntius later reformed the calendar, he moved the New Year to David's Day on the first of March, and Welsh New Year celebrations are famous to this day, above all in Deganny. Meneva is now the city of Tythew (Welsh *Tyðewi*, "David's house").

### Next to the Drengot Empire
In 1025 a series of deaths and judiciously worded treaties made Cadwallan, king of Wynneth, the first ruler of a united Wales. He faced Markland under Oswin the Becoming across the Severn. Soon afterwards Greater Devon helped Roger Drengot of Normandy to the throne of [[Kent]] in the [[Kernovan Conjuring]], and within a decade Roger was styled Emperor over most of the land from Edinbrough to the Pyrenees (the [[First Drengot Empire]]).

Wales nonetheless remained mostly free of Drengot control, steering a course between Greater Devon and the Drengot, neither of whom wanted the other to rule it. The arrangement nearly collapsed in 1121, when [[Vigo VII of Greater Devon|Vigo VII]] of Devon invaded the Tremonnow region without the blessing of Emperor Roger II, and failed. [[Dunclothe]] drew closer to the newly united Wales, as allies against Drengot power.

### Expansion
Some decades after the Drengot Empire disintegrated, Alan VI split Devon from Kernow in the realignment of 1225, and Wales spent the rest of the thirteenth century collecting counties. The armies of King David IV fought King August for the towns of the Severn estuary, and even Bristow was taken from Wessern rule for a decade or more around 1270. In 1280 Ivan, lord of Cartaw, conquered what remained of Devon, apparently on his own initiative. King David accepted the conquest, declared the Tallath of Devon, and granted it to Ivan's son Marduth.

The new tallath reignited the usual tensions with Markland. In 1301 [[Andrew II of Markland]] sacked Car Vantel (Rexam), restarting the [[Mallor Wars]], which raged again through the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries. The contested border city still has two unrelated names, Rexam and Car Vantel.

### Salt and sail
In the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries the cod fisheries of [[Dunclothe]] supplied the [[Middlesea]] markets, and the trade in salt and fish brought coin into the region on a scale unmatched since Roman times. This wealth bought Wales a new harbour at Aberdaglethow, a fishing village that by 1420 had become the second-largest port in Albion after [[London]]. In the 1400s Wales and [[Vascony]] began to monopolise trade coming from the Middlesea and [[Africa]], at the expense of [[Borland]], and the cities of the outer Welsh tallaths drew business from the port of [[Axbane]].

The same trade gave the nobility of Wales, Dunclothe, Sodrick, Kernow, Devon and the eastern Irish coast the habit of thinking of themselves as having common political interests. The united Kingdom of Britain grew out of the Tythew Compact, made at Tythew in the spring of 1424; one history calls its formation "in some ways … an accident".

### Kingdom of Britain
The new kingdom began the [[Wars of Fealty]] from a position of strength. Its victory at the [[Battle of Rexam (1483)|Battle of Rexam]] in 1483 extended the borders of Wales in the north and killed the last Duke of Chester, after which Britain stayed out of the wars' later conflicts.

## Language
The Welsh language is called Cambrick. Borlish has borrowed several words from it, such as *mordeth* "crusade, raid", *tramou* "vector, shortfall" (from Middle Cambrick *tramwi* "movement, transit") and *haval* "doll, model". [[Wessern]], the language of Britain's English minority, is spoken mainly in eastern Devon and along the lower Severn, and [[Merch]] is spoken in eastern Wales.

## Places
- [[Tremonnow]] (Welsh *Trevonwy*), a town in the south-east, where the [[Brethin]] order was founded
- Tythew (Welsh *Tyðewi*), formerly Meneva, where Saint David died
- Deganny, known for its New Year celebrations
- Aberdaglethow, the harbour built on fish and salt money
- Cartaw, whose lord Ivan conquered Devon
- Rexam, also called Car Vantel, on the border with Markland
- Mondwell, partitioned between Wynneth and Sodrick
