Britain (Cambrick **Prydein**) is a polity of [[Albion]] whose heartland is [[Wales]]. Its outlying tallaths are, roughly in order of population, [[Ireland]] to the west, the [[Devon]] peninsula and [[Kernow]] to the south, [[Dunclothe]] and [[Sodrick]] to the north, and several islands further afield. The kingdom was founded in 1424 with the Tythew Compact. Visitors are likely to be told, a few drinks in, that Britain is the oldest kingdom in Albion; correcting them is usually unwise.

## History
### The first millennium
Rome conquered the whole of [[Borland]] and never attempted Ireland, but its half-measure conquest of Albion left the Britons of the north and west with a few roads, several hundred words from Latin, and a lasting distrust of their south-easterly neighbours. Soon after Rome's departure English kingdoms arrived, conquering as far as Gaulford in Devon by the seventh century and driving some Britons across the channel to Kernow. The reversal came with [[Vigo the Magnificent|Vigo]] of Kernow, who married Winmar of Wynneth around 725 and spent the next two decades reconquering Devon.

Raids during the [[Dane Supremacy]] mostly affected the British periphery: the Landvennick monastery was burned in 888, and Sodrick and Dunclothe were settled and ruled by Danes in the tenth century. Some Welshmen went raiding in their turn, among them the romance character Owen of Jolliad, by turns friend and enemy of the Arthurians, who seems to originate in the historical Owain ap Blethen. Wynneth emerged from the Dane Supremacy as the strongest Welsh kingdom, probably funded by the Dane traffic between Dublin and Yorwick. It absorbed Powes, split Mondwell with Sodrick, and by the year 1000 was the core of a united Wales.

### Saint David
The Welsh bishop David died at Meneva, now the city of Tythew (from the Welsh for "David's house"), on the first of March towards the end of the seventh century. He was canonised under Pope Agatho (1001–26), possibly as symbolic support for the new Welsh kingdom, which was losing land to [[Markland]] and Greater Devon. A century later Pope Nuntius moved the New Year from the Annunciation on 25 March to David's Day on 1 March, and the Welsh have since celebrated the New Year more ferociously than almost any other people. Visitors to Deganny for the holiday are advised to book a bed by the previous autumn and to expect to lose two nights' sleep.

### Next to the Drengot Empire
In 1025 a series of deaths and judiciously worded treaties made Cadwallan of Wynneth the first person to rule over a united Wales. Across the Severn lay Markland under Oswin the Becoming, and in 1028 Greater Devon threw in with Roger Drengot of [[Normandy]] to install him on the throne of [[Kent]]. Within ten years Roger was styled emperor, and most of the land from Edinbrough to the Pyrenees was his domain. Wales was permitted to hold itself, an arrangement that nearly collapsed in 1121, when [[Vigo VII of Greater Devon|Vigo VII]] of Devon launched a failed invasion of the Tremonnow region without the emperor's blessing, while [[Roger II, Drengot Emperor|Roger II]] was in Paris facing a depleted treasury and a Flemish impasse.

### Wales expands
Alan VI split Devon from Kernow in the realignment of 1225, some decades after the [[First Drengot Empire]] disintegrated, and Wales spent the rest of the thirteenth century collecting counties; around 1270 it also took Bristow from [[Wessex]] for a decade or more. In 1280 Ivan of Cartaw conquered what remained of Devon, apparently on his own initiative. His king, David, showed rare good sense and decided he had authorised the act all along: he declared the Tallath of Devon and granted it to Ivan's son Marduth, Ivan having died of a fever before he could enjoy his spoils.

### Salt and sail
Through the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, while the [[Mallor Wars]] with Markland raged once more, the fishermen of Dunclothe trawled the northern banks for cod. Reaching the hungry [[Middlesea]] markets required salt for curing, and lords grew wealthy on the flow of salt north and fish south, accumulating coin on a scale unmatched since Roman times. It bought Wales a new harbour at Aberdaglethow, a fishing village that by 1420 was the second-largest port in Albion after London. More consequentially, the nobility of Wales, Dunclothe, Sodrick, Kernow, Devon and the eastern Irish coast acquired the habit of thinking of themselves as having common political interests. British fishing also indirectly brought about the [[Novomundane Landfall]] of 1471, since it was Vascony's wish to displace the British in the fish trade that led to the [[New Navarre Enterprise]].

### The Tythew Compact
The formation of a united Kingdom of Britain was in some ways an accident. On a spring day in 1424 at Tythew, the lords of the tallaths, who had spent a generation learning that they needed an ultimate authority (and scapegoat) for the navy and its contracts, founded a kingdom when they formed a unified fleet, formalising the suzerainty of Wales over the tallaths. The tallaths kept their own courts, levies, taxes and languages, and gave the crown only their navies, their coinage and their ambassadors. The final oaths were sworn on David's Day, making the New Year, the patron's day and the founding of the kingdom a single festival.

### The Wars of Fealty
The [[Wars of Fealty]] (1467–1513) began over the Redstone Chair, a throne to which Britain had neither claim nor use. Britain nonetheless joined from a position of strength, spent the early years extracting concessions from Markland, and profited greatly from the wars. After the last Duke of Chester was killed at the [[Battle of Rexam (1483)|Battle of Rexam]] in 1483 and the attack on Chester itself was rebuffed, Britain, with the border more or less where it wanted it, bowed out, leaving the rest of Albion to fight on for another thirty years. The royal marriages of the period were prestigious: [[Margaret of Iscombe]] married Prince [[Mark of Tolose]], heir to [[Vascony]], so that [[Ambrose III of Vascony|Ambrose the Ascendant]] and his sister [[Alexandra of Vascony|Alexandra]] had a Devoner mother, and Alexandra's second marriage was into Wales.

### Howel's road
[[Howel of Britain|King Howel]] spent the peace that followed on harbours, and by the turn of the century a chain of British victualling stations ran down the Atlantic coast of [[Africa]], built to reach Asia directly by sea. The *[[Voyage of the Phœbos|Phœbos]]* followed that road to [[Borune]], where in 1517 it met the Morrack ship *Cynthia* at the [[Great Borunesk Meeting]]. While the poorer polities of Albion were left vulnerable to the [[Second Great Dying]], Britain's economic success let direct sea trade between [[Europe]] and Asia flourish.

### Later history
The next British ships to cross an ocean went west. The seventeenth century saw the dawn of British exploration of the [[Novomund]], founding staddomains along the Arcabil Gulf. These would coalesce into several polities known collectively as [[British Mendeva]] over the next centuries, the largest of which is [[Hasiny]]. Britain here found itself in conflict with [[Provence]]'s settlements on the east coast; for example, the [[Sack of Santrafew]] was undertaken by an alliance of British and Mastoisèt forces.

After the [[Angelican Landfall]], emigration from Britain (along with more formal state-building enterprise from [[Cathay]]) helped form the modern polity of [[New Leudong]].

In the latter years of the nineteenth century, [[British Mendeva]] overtook Britain in population and industry. Nonetheless, Britain retained some cultural prestige over the Novomundane polities. In part this was sustained via migration of many members of the 'new rich' across the Atlantic, and the resultant intertwining of the respective upper-class societies through marriage. It is debated whether this prestige accelerated the export of [[Household Renovation]] social mores from Britain to Mendeva.
