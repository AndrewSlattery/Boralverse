Crain (Bavarn /krɐːn/, Borlish **Carniol** /karˈnjɔl/) is a region of Central [[Europe]], east of [[Furlain]], north of Crovatia and south of [[Ruritania]]. In the Middle Ages it was a march and then a duchy of the [[First German Empire|German Empire]], prosperous through trade into its coastal city of Trieste.

## History
### The German Empire
During the [[Second Tetrarchy]] of the eleventh and twelfth centuries, Crain was a march in the southeasternmost part of the German Empire, at first autonomous but later subordinate to the Kingdom of [[Bavarn]]. Its prosperity came mostly from trade into Trieste, and it also held fertile lands further east. The [[Convoy Australier]], officially the Order of Saint Hemma of Still Water, began in the Duchy of Crain in the eleventh century, at first as little more than a glorified band of mercenaries.

### The German Secession
During the [[German Secession]] of the thirteenth century, when many polities broke with the Roman Pope in favour of the hastily established [[Augsburg See]], the duchy declared for the [[Roman Papacy]]. This was likely more from pragmatism than religious fervour, as its western and southern neighbours, Furlain and Crovatia, were both partisans of Rome. Whatever its motives, Crain became an important Roman stronghold and was perfectly placed to contribute to the [[Baptism of the North]]. Forces from Crain and [[Borland]], with Borlish missionaries and the Convoy Australier, converged on their first target, [[Lithow]], and went on to bring Christianity to its more distant Graven neighbours.
