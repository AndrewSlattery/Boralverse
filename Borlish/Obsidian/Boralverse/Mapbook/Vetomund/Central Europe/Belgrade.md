Belgrade (Borlish **Belgrad**; adjective *belgradian*) is a city on the Danaw. It gave its name to the [[Decadence of Belgrade]], the last decades of the [[Kingdom of the Danaw]], and after the kingdom's collapse it was one of the [[Riverine Cities]]. In 1850 it hosted the committee that produced the [[Laic Declarations]], the landmark accord for secular government.

## History
### The Kingdom of the Danaw
The kingdom's final decades before the [[Second German War]] (1815–23), contemporary with the [[Long Peace]], are known as the Decadence of Belgrade. In this period the kingdom was at its height in territory and foreign relations, having taken [[Bulgary]] and pushed back its border with [[Bavarn]], even as its internal economy and stability declined.

### The Riverine Cities
After the kingdom collapsed, Belgrade became one of the Riverine Cities, an alliance-federacy of small independent states along the Danaw and its tributaries. It was an early hotbed of new religious and legal thinking, with a renowned Promethean club associated with the [[Deviance movement]] and a semi-public tovaric community.

### The Laic Declarations
Belgrade was accordingly an obvious host for the international committee, made up of delegations from several dozen polities, that produced the Laic Declarations in 1850. They negotiated the unyoking of religious and administrative authority, and their passing is largely credited to the strong influence of Anti-Paradise theology in the Riverine Cities. Both the Deviance movement and the [[Borrhatic League]] contributed wording to them. Only one Novomundane polity sent a delegation to the signing.

The Declarations' anniversaries have been marked in and beyond the city. Their centenary was celebrated in Belgrade with a week-long event beginning on 2 December 1950, opened by a keynote speech from the political theorist Maya Tavares of the de Sarumptice Diplomatic Edifice. For the hundred-and-twentieth anniversary in 1970, the Long Future project saw academies publish dozens of popular works on the Declarations simultaneously, and as part of the celebrations more than a dozen polities passed laws at the same time.
