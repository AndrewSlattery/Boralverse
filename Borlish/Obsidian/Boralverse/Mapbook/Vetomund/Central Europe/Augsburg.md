Augsburg (Borlish **Augsbourg**; adjective *Augsburgher*, Borlish *augsbourger*) is a German city, the seat of the [[Augsburg See]], the papal line that broke from Rome in the [[German Secession]] of the thirteenth century.

## History
For at least a decade in the first half of the thirteenth century the city's mayor was Wilhelm Brüz, a personal friend of Paul von Burchardt, the future first pope of the Augsburg See. Brüz's ambitions for his city are thought to have influenced some of the religious policy in the [[Pauline Denunciations]] of 1225. In a 1961 film's version of a letter he wrote to Paul in June of that year, he asks whether the Lord can truly have ordained Rome, "the ancient seat of the Christ-killers themselves", to be the most holy city in the world. That year Paul, as [[Paul I/XI]], declared a *sedes sanctissima* in Augsburg, and in the decades that followed each duchy of the [[First German Empire]] declared either for Rome or for Augsburg.

In the fourteenth century a cathedral was being built at Augsburg which, along with another at Valencia, might have surpassed Saint Sophia's in Stamboul as the largest in Christendom, had Saint Sophia's not been expanded under the architect Nicolà s Avignon.

## Landmarks
- The town hall (Rathaus), outside which a magnified statue of Brüz was erected in 1975. It is not based on any extant description or depiction of him, since none exists.

## In culture
Brüz was played by Hunter Noteworthy in the 1961 historical epic *With the North Star to Guide Us*, a dramatisation of the Secession.
