The Middlesea (Borlish **Miterran**) is the sea between [[Europe]] to the north and [[Africa]] to the south, connected to the [[Atlantic]] in the west.

## Coasts
- In [[Spain]]: [[Andalus]], including the port of Tarifa, and [[Barcelon]] in the east
- In [[Gaul]]: [[Provence]], with its capital [[Marsella]]
- In [[Italy]]: [[Jeans]] on the north-western coast, [[Tuscany]] on the Tyrrhene Sea, and [[Venise]]
- The Tyrrhene Isles of Corse and Sardine
- In Africa: [[Morrack]], [[Libya]] and [[Egypt]]. The African coast was long held by the [[Single Caliphate]], seated at Cairo, and Trapoles in Libya was a major Middlesea port of the Caliphate during the [[Second Tetrarchy]].

## History
### Crusades and republics
Between the eleventh and thirteenth centuries, several wars of conquest were waged in the Middlesea far from home, under varying degrees of Papal authority. Borlish calls these expeditions *mordeth*, a word borrowed from Welsh *mordeith* "sea voyage, raid".

The mercantile republics of the Middlesea, among them [[Jeans]], [[Venise]] and (from 1497) [[Provence]], built great trade networks across the sea; one history of them covers the years 1000 to 1800. In the thirteenth century Provence became the direct link between the Burgundian capital at Geneva and all of the Middlesea sea-trade, and was known as *lo panier de Borgogne*, "the Burgundian breadbasket".

### The northern trade
In the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries the North Atlantic cod fisheries of [[Dunclothe]] sold their catch as far as the eastern Middlesea. Salt for curing flowed north and fish flowed south. [[Vascony]], whose kingdom linked the republican trade networks of the Middlesea, founded the [[New Navarre Enterprise]] in 1458 to solidify Vascon supremacy in that fish trade. In the north, the port of [[Axbane]] was thought to handle over a third of European trade outside the Middlesea by 1400. By the late fifteenth century, [[Damvath]] in [[Borland]] was said to be the busiest port city outside the Middlesea.

### Piracy and the Novomund
After the [[Novomundane Landfall]] of 1471, piracy thrived on the general prosperity. It had long plagued the coast that made Provence and the republics rich, and in Marsella the raiding became so frequent that many of the wealthy fled inland. Piracy peaked at the turn of the sixteenth century as wealth from the [[Novomund]] flooded the region. In the early sixteenth century Barcelon funded piratical raids on the cities of newly independent Provence. The fleets of Provence employed mariners from across the Middlesea, and some of them formed communities in [[Santrafew]], the Provincial trading post in [[Mendeva]] founded in 1564.

In the seventeenth century Libya warred intermittently with Morrack over control of trade into the eastern Middlesea. The Bordew-Audaz canal, completed in 1679, joined two rivers of Gaul and so connected the Atlantic to the Middlesea without passing through Morrack-controlled seas.

## Culture
- Rebound is a wall-and-ball game played around the coast. Any city or town worthy of the name has a rebound wall, though there is no unified ruleset.
- The 1880 mealbook *Pasti Mezziterreni* (*Middlesea Meals*), by the traveller Josforo Sant'Angelo, gathers recipes from around the sea. It is arranged as a single sunward journey along the whole coast, from Tarifa in Andalus to Ẋr l Joas in Morrack.
- In the sixteenth-century Revitalist period, scholars and artists across the Middlesea looked back to the works and beliefs of Classical Greece and Rome.
