Africa is one of the three continents of the [[Vetomund]], lying south of [[Europe]] across the [[Middlesea]].

## Name
The name has its roots in Latin *Āfer*, usually taken to be from a Punic name for the people related to *afar* "dust".

## North Africa
The Middlesea coast was long held by the [[Single Caliphate]], seated at Cairo. As imperial hegemony in Europe and northern Africa fell apart, independent admiralties were declared in Libya and the Maghreb in 1237.

- [[Morrack]], primarily in the north-west of the continent
- [[Libya]], between Morrack and Egypt
- [[Egypt]], whose port of Iscandar was the western anchor of the Riyalid Sultanate in the seventeenth century

## East Africa
- Hadramot, a loose collection of wealthy city states and admiralties in Yemen and Ethiopia, controlling trade through the Gulf of Aden
- [[Ungotia]], a trading port of long standing, where Suahile is spoken

## West Africa
- Bamackon, a major population centre of the fourteenth century, suzerain over parts of the coast to its south. The Bamackon languages gave European languages *gorray* "kola nut".
- Sant Adrian, an island under Vascon control since the fifteenth century

## Southern Africa
- Caddisow, whose opening in the mid-nineteenth century is blamed in part for the decline of the [[Voidtale|voidtale]] craze

## Voyages and trade
- In the fifteenth century [[Vascony]] explored the western coast as far south as Sant Adrian. In 1458 King [[Ambrose III of Vascony|Ambrose III]] founded the [[New Navarre Enterprise]], partly to create trade ports along that coast.
- In 1468 the ship *Orion* of [[Morrack]]'s [[New World Company]] circumnavigated Africa.
- [[Britain]] under King [[Howel of Britain|Howel]] set up resupply ports down the western coast. Its ship the [[Voyage of the Phœbos|Phœbos]] rounded Africa on its way to [[Borune]] in 1516.
- Borlish trade guilds helped establish staddomains along the Atlantic coast. These grew from trading posts into significant polities, whose inhabitants are called staddenzen.
- In the seventeenth century the trade empire of [[Gurjat]] reached African ports such as Ungotia and Sant Adrian.
