Spain (Borlish **Spagn**) is the peninsula in the south-west of [[Europe]], south of [[Gaul]] and across the straits from [[Morrack]] in [[Africa]]. Its polities include [[Vascony]] in the north, which also reaches into southern Gaul, [[Portingale]] in the west, [[Barcelon]] in the east and [[Andalus]] in the south.

## Medieval Spain
The [[Single Caliphate]] stretched from Spain to Persia, though distant territories such as Cordova were never as closely ruled as its heartland. In the ninth century Andalus saw a flowering of theoretic advances, notably in glassmaking. During the eleventh and twelfth centuries it lost significant territory in the north and east to Christian polities, among them the newly established Kingdom of Barcelon (a kingdom from 1067). Portingale became an independent Christian kingdom in the eleventh century, having previously been a county of Leon, and Vascony, formed from the union of Aquitain and Navarre, made Leon its vassal in the twelfth.

A mapbook of the period shows the peninsula shortly after the Aragonese conquest of Toledo in 1198 divided among five powers:
- the Kingdom of Vascony, under Ranolf VI (crowned 1170), with its court at Bordew and other centres at Lorda, Bilvaw and Leon
- the Duchy of [[Tolose]], under James (crowned 1191), with its court at Tolosa
- the Kingdom of Portingale, under Eustace II (crowned 1151), with its court at Port Cale and other centres at Lisbon and Corunna
- the Kingdom of Barcelon, under Martha II (crowned 1186), with its court at Barcelon and other centres at Saragossa, Valencia and Toledo
- the Admiralty of Cordova, under Abdul Raman IV (crowned 1188), with its court at Cordova and other centres at Seville, Murcia and Malaga

## The Vascon Ascendancy
In the fifteenth century Vascony was ascendant. During the [[Vascon Ascendancy]], [[Ambrose II of Vascony|Ambrose II]] conquered northern Portingale as far south as Braga and (nominally) Calès, and the rump of Portingale fell to Morrack in 1457. Andalus, meanwhile, was controlled from across the straits by Morrack and beset by two successive succession crises and the 1457 Lisbon Massacre.

In 1458 [[Ambrose III of Vascony|Ambrose III]] founded the [[New Navarre Enterprise]], whose ships sighted [[Brasil]] in 1471 and brought news of the [[Novomund]] to Europe. The wealth that followed the [[Novomundane Landfall]] accrued above all to the coastal towns of Vascon Portingale and to Leon. In the first decade of the sixteenth century Barcelon took Tolose, the last of Vascony's Middlesea coast.

## Early modern Spain
The [[Second Great Dying]] reached Europe in 1519, and coastal Portingale lost a fifth of its population within a decade. In the [[Romantic Wars]] of the mid-seventeenth century Vascony lost Portingale, its economic heartland, to Andalus; these losses delayed the Bordew-Audaz canal through Gaul, finally completed in 1679. In the eighteenth century Andalus fought a prolonged war against an attempted reconquest by Morrack, during which a signalling line was set up from Cordova to Málaga; it later developed into the world's first [[Steeplepost|steeplepost]] connection between two major cities. Portingale tried to stay neutral in the [[Democratic Wars]], but after the All-Saints' earthquake of 1755 its neighbours invaded.

## Modern Spain
The [[Modest Arrangement]] saw a resurgence in Spain and Gaul in the early nineteenth century. The [[Masquira romance|masquira]] genre originated in Spain; it takes its name from the 1860 trevold *Tejan de Masquira ou Jalico* by the Portingale author [[Ezio Carvallo]], and it flourished in [[Borland]] later in the century. In the twentieth century Portingale entered the orbit of the [[Drengot Collusion]] without becoming a member, which strained its relations with Vascony, which lies between them.

## Languages
Languages of the peninsula include Vascon and Vask in Vascony, Portingale, Barcelon, and the Spanish languages such as Mostara Spanish, which is widely spoken in Andalus alongside Morrove Arabic. The northern Spanish languages share a characteristic sound shift of *f* to *h*. Borlish has borrowed several words from Spanish, often of Arabic origin, such as *amaçgat* "nursery" and *cadarar* "to distil".
