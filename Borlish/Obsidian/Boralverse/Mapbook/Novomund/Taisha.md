Taisha (Borlish **Taisca**) is a polity of [[Gulf Mendeva]], bordered by [[Hasiny]] to the east and [[Mashick]] to the south. Its coastline stretches from east of the Tocolon to southwest of the Nar Mottare, and its capital is Yanaquana. A minor polity at the crossroads of British and [[Morrack]] influences, it became independent in 1936.

## Toponymy
The name Taisha comes via Arabic *Tayisha'* from Carovacian *taísha'* "welcome". It was first written down by Morrack cartographers of the [[New World Company]] in the early sixteenth century, and it is presumed that the word was simply the most common formula the expedition heard on its voyage.

## History
### Before the Concordat
Until the seventeenth century the eastern half of modern Taisha was part of the Hasiny confederacy of Carovacian peoples, from which the neighbouring polity takes its name. The southern and western reaches were under the influence of a series of Mashick vassals, trading upriver along the Tocolon and across the interior.

Taisha's first Vetomundane contact was with Morrack's New World Company in the 1510s. Through the later sixteenth century Mashick expanded northeast along the coast, establishing frontier towns, but its authority in the far northeast remained thin. From the middle of the seventeenth century, British and Jaughta ranchers and merchants, with the tacit imprimatur of the Kingdom of [[Britain]], moved steadily west into the Tocolon basin. For most of the eighteenth century the region had three overlapping structures of governance: Mashick regents in the south and west, Hasinic towns along the coast, and Carovacian councils in the interior.

### The Concordat of Aberdocolon
The Concordat of Aberdocolon (1850), signed in the port city of that name, formalised this arrangement between the Mashick crown, the newly autonomous Hasinic crown and elected Carovacian leaders. Its practical effect was minimal, and its commissioners spent most of the following decades arbitrating cattle-rustling disputes. Later Taishan historiography nonetheless elevated it into a founding document in which the region's three communities were recognised as co-equal parties. In 1950 a Taishan centenary celebration was held at one of its border cities.

### Mashick province
In the late nineteenth century Mashick reasserted central control and reorganised the country as the northeasternmost province of the Mashick Empire. This was also the great age of Taishan [[Herdtale romance|herdtale]] literature, the genre having by then cooled in Hasiny itself. The grounded and unsentimental stories from Taisha are generally considered the pinnacle of the genre.

### Independence
The [[Lovac Wars]] of the 1920s and 1930s brought economic collapse, two cycles of conscription, and the first bombardment of Yanaquana by airship. In 1936 a loose self-rule coalition, drawing explicitly on the Concordat's tripartite model, issued the Declaration of Yanaquana. Mashick, undergoing its own Ubiquitous revolution, did not contest it and granted Taisha legal independence that same year.
