Gulf Mendeva is the region of [[Mendeva]] made up of the polities along the Arcabil Gulf (Borlish *Bugt Arcabil*), on the continent's southern coast. Most of [[British Mendeva]] lies within it.

## Geography
The Arcabil Gulf lies east of [[Lower Mendeva]], and the islands of the [[Arcabil]] lie within it. The [[Bahassassin]], the longest river in Mendeva, flows south through [[Hasiny]] into the gulf. [[New Provence]] borders the states of Gulf Mendeva to its south.

## Polities
- [[Hasiny]], the largest and most populous polity of British Mendeva, with its capital at [[Nadacow]] on the lower Bahassassin
- [[Taisha]], a minor polity at the crossroads of British and [[Morrack]] influences, bordered by Hasiny to the east and [[Mashick]] to the south, with its capital at Yanaquana

## History
Before the seventeenth century the Hasiny confederacy of Carovacian peoples held lands that are now part of Hasiny and Taisha. Morrack cartographers of the [[New World Company]] wrote down the name Taisha in the early sixteenth century. In the seventeenth century, exploratory voyages by ships of the Kingdom of [[Britain]] founded staddomains along the Arcabil Gulf, which grew by settlement, treaty and conquest into the polities of British Mendeva.

British Mendeva overtook Britain in population and industry in the late nineteenth century, but Britain kept its cultural cachet in Gulf Mendeva. The ethos of the [[Household Renovation]] spread there especially through that cachet. Britain's persistent social prestige is also thought by some to have hastened the social changes that came with the adoption of the [[Laic Declarations]] in the region, though the timeline of Laic adoption in Gulf Mendeva is still debated.

In 1921 educational institutions across Gulf Mendeva affiliated with the fi Narcìs Normal Primers, headquartered in Nadacow, offered a course on Mendevan transport networks in the nineteenth century. After the [[Lovac Wars]], the Mashick author and one-time spy Yollamaniza Elcabir depicted the Mashick and Hasinic Ministries of Knowledge so faithfully in his 1943 spycraft novel *La te Hozuho Sineton* (*No Slumber Will Seize Him*) that governments across Gulf Mendeva issued warrants for his arrest. In 1950 Provence had an ambassador to Gulf Mendeva, Matties Gramunt.
