The Blue Trans-Willem War (Willem *Blaw Trans-Willemsk Órlog*; Borlish **Var Tras-Willem Blau**) was one of a series of early modern wars fought principally between French and Dutch polities, the powers on either side of [[Willemy]], and waged de facto for control of that territory. It ended in the late seventeenth century, and its most lasting result was the dissolution of the Kingdom of [[Burgundy]].

## Periodisation
The sporadic flare-ups of fighting over Willemy are difficult to divide into discrete wars without some arbitrariness. Modern historiography usually follows the chromatic periodisation of the nineteenth-century writer Andrysk de Santer, whose colours are said to come either from the victors' standards or from alchemical symbolism. In her scheme the Blue war is the penultimate conflict. It is distinct from both the White and the Red Trans-Willem Wars. Some historians, though not de Santer, count the Blue war among the [[Romantic Wars]] of the mid-seventeenth century.

## Course
Willemy was the focal point of an especially bloody war. The duchy of [[Avosche]], with its seat at Axton, had been a stable French vassal through the sixteenth century, but changed hands five times in the seventeenth, most bloodily in the Blue war.

## Aftermath
After a series of smaller humiliations in earlier decades, Burgundy suffered severe losses to neighbouring [[Bavarn]], and as a coup de grâce the kingdom and the title of King of Burgundy were formally dissolved. Its territory was partitioned between [[Provence]] and the kingdoms of [[France]] and Bavarn, with Bavarn gaining significant territory. King Frederick of Bavarn created the Duchy of Far Suebia, and the former Burgundian capital, Geneva, became a subordinate city within it. Avosche was returned to French control as part of French Guillenne.
