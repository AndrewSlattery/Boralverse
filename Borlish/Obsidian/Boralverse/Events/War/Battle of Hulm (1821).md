The Battle of Hulm (Borlish **Battagl a Houlm**) was an offensive of the [[Second German War]] fought at Hulm in 1821. It is remembered as disastrously mismanaged, and one veteran believed it prolonged the war by months.

## Combatants
[[Borland]], allied in the war with [[Bavarn]] and the [[Northern Concert]], sent soldiers to Hulm. They fought alongside Bavarn troops and those of many other nearby countries, among them [[Crovatia]] and [[Furlain]]. Furlain's presence on this side is notable, as it changed sides during the war: elsewhere in the conflict its troops fought principally against Bavarn.

## Remembrance
The Borlish mathematician Darren Brodus, who fought at Hulm, called it in a letter of 1824 "an astonishingly-mismanaged offensive" that "in all honesty prolonged the war for months", though he refused to start a rant on the subject for fear he would never end it. Among his comrades there was [[Jacomo Bruno]], a minor landowner from Furlain and the twin brother of the librettist and composer [[Chiarina Bruno]].

At home, Borland's part in the war, and especially its alliance with the Northern Concert, sparked major mozard protests in cities across the country.
