The War of Provincial Independence (1497–99) was the war in which [[Provence]] broke away from the Kingdom of [[Burgundy]]. It began with the Provincial nobility's declaration of independence on 21 June 1497 and ended with Burgundy's defeat at the [[Battle of Margès (1499)|Battle of Margès]] two years later, which left Burgundy with no coastal territory.

## Background
Provence had been the largest province of Burgundy since the [[Emergency at Dijon]] of 1170. Under the house of [[Savoy]] it was elevated to a duchy in the thirteenth century. As the direct link between the capital at Geneva and all of [[Middlesea]] sea-trade, it was known as *lo panier de Borgogne*, the Burgundian breadbasket.

During the [[Vascon Ascendancy]], [[Vascony]] took Burgundy's Middlesea coast, including Provence. Piracy had long plagued that coast, and it thrived on the prosperity that followed the [[Novomundane Landfall]] of 1471. In [[Marsella]] the raiding became so frequent that many treated it more as an occupation, and many of the wealthy fled inland. Burgundy won Provence back from Vascony in 1494, during the declining later years of [[Ambrose III of Vascony|Ambrose III]], but the reconquest caused so much instability that the region rose in revolt.

Provence's requests for aid to the young king [[Rudolf VIII, King of Burgundy|Rudolf VIII]] went largely unanswered. Charitably, he may have been distracted by the ongoing conflict with [[Bavarn]], and by the religious difficulty of being the northwesternmost polity still answerable to [[Roman Papacy|Rome]]. It is generally allowed, however, that he cared only for himself, his many lovers and his lakeside banquets.

## Declaration
On the advice of the foundling-general [[Guillen daus Sanz]], Lord [[Daniel Menton]] of the house of Amigòt secured the aid of King [[Marc V of France]]. France had at last reached a reconciliation with Normandy, avoiding the tangle of the [[Wars of Fealty]] in [[Albion]]. With this support, Menton and several other noble families declared independence from Geneva on 21 June 1497. The day is still a national holiday in Provence, and Marsella's midsummer celebrations also mark the anniversary.

## Battle of Margès
The declaration was not formally recognised until Burgundy's defeat at the Battle of Margès in 1499. On the eve of the battle daus Sanz is popularly said to have mocked the states of Albion, which had by then been at war with one another for two decades:

> Perhaps we should have sent for a Kentish witness! They might learn the subtle craft of ending the wars they start.

## Aftermath
The declaration cemented House Amigòt's position as *primus inter pares* in the new republic, whose legislature, the Concert, was modelled on that of the Venisians. Menton led Provence for the next eighteen years, manoeuvring against a hungry [[Barcelon]] and its piratical allies, who ruled Marsella de facto. He is generally understood to have made Marsella the capital largely to keep watch on Jondumal's fleets. By the end of his rule in 1517 he had settled the borders with Burgundy and Barcelon, leaving the region of Narbona with double obligations to Menton and to King James III (Jaume III) of Barcelon.

Burgundy, having lost its coast to Provence, weathered the [[Second Great Dying]] from 1519 comparatively well, since the plague spread chiefly by naval trade. For Vascony, the loss of Provence was followed a decade later by the loss of the rest of its Middlesea coast to Barcelon.
