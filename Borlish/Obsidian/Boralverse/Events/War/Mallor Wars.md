The Mallor Wars (Borlish **Rix Mallor**) is the collective name for the perennial, on-again off-again conflicts between [[Markland]] and [[Wales]].

## History
Around the turn of the second millennium Markland was conquering territory from the northern reaches of Wales, which incidentally led Pope Agatho to canonise the Welsh Saint David. By the thirteenth century tensions between the two had been high for almost a millennium.

After a quiescent period, the martial-minded King [[Andrew II of Markland]], who had succeeded his more diplomatic uncle, reignited the wars in 1301 with the [[Sack of Rexam]], the town also known as Car Vantel. The original medieval building of St Brigid's Abbey burned to the ground during the sack. The wars raged once more through the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, and it is not for nothing that the city has two unrelated names, Rexam and Car Vantel. Meanwhile Wales, enriched by the fishing and salt trade of the period, built a new harbour at Aberdaglethow, and its nobility came to think of themselves as sharing political interests with those of Dunclothe, Sodrick, Kernow, Devon and the eastern Irish coast, out of which the Kingdom of [[Britain]] was formed in 1424.
