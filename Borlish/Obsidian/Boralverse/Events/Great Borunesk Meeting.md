The Great Borunesk Meeting (Borlish **Gran Rajognt Borunesc**) was the gathering of 1517 at Sinquan, on the island of [[Borune]], where the British ship *[[Voyage of the Phœbos|Phœbos]]*, which had sailed east around [[Africa]], met the [[Morrack]] ship *Cynthia*, which had sailed west by way of [[Cappatia]]. Morrack ships thereby became the first to circumnavigate the world. The tale of the twin voyages is still counted among the greatest stories of exploration and discovery, measured against Homer's *Odyssey* and Quoming's *The Sea Peoples*.

## Background
### The fair at Sinquan
The Meeting was not convened in honour of its British and Morrack guests. At least a century before any merchant ship had rounded Africa, an annual fair had become a tradition at Sinquan, on Borune's northwestern coast. Each year the city filled with traders from [[China]], [[Gurjat]] and further afield, who stayed until the rain-winds changed and let them sail home.

### The *Phœbos*
Having profited from the [[Wars of Fealty]] raging across the rest of [[Albion]], King [[Howel of Britain|Howel]] of [[Britain]], whose ships had already set up resupply ports along the African coast, was convinced that more opportunity lay in connecting the European market directly to Asia. He doubtless also hoped to displace the Borlish supremacy in the northern trade. Ships led by the *Phœbos*, captained by Martan mac Kellot, were sent to sail as far east as they could. Directed by [[Uman|Umanick]] and Chinese traders, the *Phœbos* rounded Africa, spent several months at Gurjat in 1512, called at Romeswarm in [[Madray]], and made port at Borune in 1516, then almost the southeastern extremity of the known [[Vetomund]].

### The *Cynthia*
The *Cynthia* sailed under Fredun al-Casmi, a Persian immigrant to Morrack. It first made port at the Arcabil and Meridian outposts, then followed the Cappatian coast, where al-Casmi's logbooks describe the Dovachin straits and island, named from the Arabic for "smoke" after the smoke-towers he saw the islanders build. Entering the southern Lovac Ocean, the ship sailed north along the coast before turning west into the open sea. At midsummer 1515 it put in at Jazirat Asnam, an encounter infamous for its acrimony, and on 11 Shaban 921 the crew began to perform the salat facing forward rather than aft, once the astronomer Taisir as-Sadik judged that they had passed the longitude of the antipode of Mecca. The next spring the ship reached an archipelago almost recognisable as Asia, which was hence named Asia Cahal, now Cahalla.

At [[Rhalodony]], al-Casmi recorded a local legend of a Fisher-King who had raised a great land from the sea, and of its direction. The *Cynthia* sailed southwest and after sixteen days reached [[Tecamaw]], where, after a month of tensions, the people welcomed the arrivals as long-lost kin of Havechine. In 1516 the ship left northbound with two men of Tecamaw aboard, made landfall at [[Bidia]], and at last loaded long-sought cloves and other spices.

## The Meeting
The *Cynthia* reached Borune in Muharram 924 after travelling further west than anyone, quite possibly, in human history. Much of the island had converted to Islam over the previous few decades through continual trade with Muslim India, and the crew heard, in Arabic, stories of map-makers from the Far West. They followed the coast to Sinquan and found the *Phœbos* at anchor there.

The meeting of the two ships is the definitive moment of the Meeting. In the 1927 film *One Day in Borune*, mac Kellot's first mate hails the *Cynthia* with *O Gastor ‘ila Bolux!*, "From Castor to Pollux!", after the celestial twins of Greek myth; whether this happened is unknown, since al-Casmi's otherwise impeccable logbook is missing the pages around the date. It is popularly assumed that the two crews could not understand one another. In fact more than a dozen of the *Phœbos*'s crew had some Arabic, since the British and Morrack mercantile domains had overlapped in Gaul and Spain for nearly two centuries. At least two of them wrote it fluently, as al-Hamid's book of circular functions, not yet translated into Latin, was an invaluable navigational aid.

The harbour rolls, registers and manifests of the gathering are now scattered among universities across the globe, and Sinquan's annals are the first record of the names of the two men of Tecamaw. The written records of the Meeting are also used as evidence for the effects on Tecamaw and Bidia of the reintroduction of long-distance sail.

## Aftermath
The *Phœbos* did not try to sail on around the globe, as tales of the size of the oceans ahead dissuaded mac Kellot. The *Cynthia* returned home. Claims from later centuries that it brought back the disease behind the [[Second Great Dying]] cannot be sustained, since the plague struck the Borlish ports months before.

Word of the Meeting travelled further than any single ship. In 1520, hearing that Morrack and Britain planned to sail round the globe, the Dinghoi Emperor of China issued the Hangthen Edict, which declared that if the world could be girdled, China should tie the ropes herself. European and North African accounts of the Meeting also brought the Bāsan word *kembang* "flower" into Borlish as *kembang* "firework".
