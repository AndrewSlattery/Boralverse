Rodulf was king of [[Kent]] for nearly eight decades until his death in the late 1020s. He came to the throne as a child, and under him Kent prospered and expanded. His death, with the succession deeply uncertain, triggered the [[Kernovan Conjuring]] of 1029, in which the Norman House of Drengot conquered Kent and began the [[First Drengot Empire]].

## Reign
By the end of Rodulf's reign Kent, ruled from Canterbury, was stable and much expanded from its position in the ninth and early tenth centuries. It had subdued [[Wessex]], apart from Anglia, which had already been lost to [[Markland]] in the Treaty of Oxford, and held a recognised supremacy over the rump Wessex, though [[Devon]] contested it with renewed Dumnonian pushes from the west. Kent's most powerful vassals lay to the north in [[Essex]], across the Thames, ably run by Alfred the Tall.

## Death and the Conjuring
In 1028 the elderly Rodulf lay on his deathbed after eight decades of political manoeuvring, and the succession was deeply uncertain. His death triggered the Kernovan Conjuring of 1029: the House of Drengot in [[Normandy]] invaded and conquered Kent, the "Petty Kingdom of Kent", with the support of Greater Devon's House of Kernow, which the Drengots then helped to install on the Devoner throne. The conquest was the first step in the creation of the First Drengot Empire and the beginning of the political union of English Kent with Normandy. Roger of Normandy went on to conquer Kent's neighbours, including Essex and Markland, and was crowned Emperor Roger I.
