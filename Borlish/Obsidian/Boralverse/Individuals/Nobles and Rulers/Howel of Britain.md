Howel was king of [[Britain]] in the late fifteenth and early sixteenth centuries. He used the wealth Britain had gained from the [[Wars of Fealty]] to build a chain of victualling stations down the Atlantic coast of [[Africa]], and sent the *[[Voyage of the Phœbos|Phœbos]]* on the voyage that reached [[Borune]] in 1516.

## Background
The Kingdom of Britain was founded with the Tythew Compact of 1424, formalising the suzerainty of [[Wales]] over the tallaths of [[Devon]] and [[Dunclothe]]. The tallaths kept their own courts, levies, taxes and languages, giving the crown only their navies, their coinage and their ambassadors. Britain joined the Wars of Fealty from a position of strength and spent their early years extracting concessions from [[Markland]]. After the last Duke of Chester was killed at the [[Battle of Rexam (1483)|Battle of Rexam]] in 1483 and the attack on Chester itself was rebuffed, Britain, with the border more or less where it wanted it, bowed out, leaving the rest of [[Albion]] to fight on for another thirty years.

## Howel's road
Howel spent the peace that followed on harbours. Having profited from the wars at the expense of the other polities of Albion, he was convinced that more opportunity lay in connecting the European market directly with Asia, and he doubtless also hoped to displace the Borlish supremacy in the northern trade, though that ambition was not achieved in his lifetime. By the turn of the century a chain of British victualling stations ran down the Atlantic coast of Africa.

Ships led by the *Phœbos*, under Martan mac Kellot, were sent to sail as far east as they could. They went down Howel's road and kept going, via Ungotia, Gurjat, where the *Phœbos* spent several months of 1512, and [[Madray]], reaching Borune in 1516. At Sinquan the following year the [[Morrack]] ship *Cynthia* found the *Phœbos* at anchor, in the [[Great Borunesk Meeting]]. Told how much ocean stretched out to the east, mac Kellot declined to sail round the world and turned back.

Britain's advantageous position after the Wars of Fealty contributed to the success of Howel's expansion of naval trade. While the poorer polities of Albion were left vulnerable to the [[Second Great Dying]], Britain's ventures in Africa eventually allowed direct sea trade between Europe and Asia to flourish.
