The New World Company (Arabic شركة العالم الجديد; Borlish **Colluy dy Nou Mont**) was a marine enterprise of [[Morrack]], founded in 1463 by Malik [[Munir al-Hamdawi of Morrack|Munir al-Hamdawi]]. Set up as a rival to the [[New Navarre Enterprise]] of his enemy [[Ambrose III of Vascony|Ambrose III]], it at first traded eastward. After the [[Novomundane Landfall]] of 1471 it turned west, and its ships made Morrack one of the earliest Vetomundane powers to explore the [[Novomund]] extensively. Its name is often called prophetic.

## History
### Eastward voyages
Five years after the Company's founding, in 1468, its ship *Orion* sailed from Anfa around [[Africa]] to Aden. On the way it made port at Vascon Sant Adrian and met Chinese merchants at Mombasa, whose reports to the Southern Emperor of [[China]] are said to have changed the course of history.

### The Novomund
Almost as soon as news of the Landfall reached Munir's court, the Company's ships were sent westward. They discovered the faster southern route to the Novomund, and in the late fifteenth century they became the first Vetomundane ships to reach the islands of the [[Arcabil]]. By the early sixteenth century Morrack had several semi-permanent outposts there, which were important ports for the circumnavigation of the *Cynthia* under Fredun al-Casmi.

Company merchants also opened contact between [[Lower Mendeva]] and the Vetomund, establishing trading relations with the relatively new and then much smaller [[Mashick]] Empire. In the following decades many Novomundane crops, among them tashban and shockle, crossed the Atlantic from Mashick. In the early sixteenth century Company cartographers first wrote down the name of [[Taisha]], from a Carovacian word meaning "welcome", and Taisha's first Vetomundane contact came with the Company in the 1510s.

Further south, Company traders were the first Vetomundane people to document the [[Tavancy|Tavancine Empire]] of [[Cappatia]]. Through the sixteenth century they established wayports along the western coast of Cappatia, some of which grew into substantial staddomains.
