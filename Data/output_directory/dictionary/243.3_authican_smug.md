243.3: authican "smug"

authican /ˌo.θiˈkan/ [ˌo.θɪˈkan]
- self-sufficient, autarkic, providing for all its own needs;
- self-contained, standalone, needing nothing outside itself to be understood or to run;
- solitary, aloof, standoffish, wanting no company;
- circular, question-begging, assuming what it sets out to prove;
- smug, complacent, self-satisfied, pleased with oneself and incurious about others

Etymology: Middle Borlish, a borrowing via Scholastic Latin authicanus from Malchassian Greek αὐθικανός • authikanós "self-sufficient", compounded from αὐτός • autós "self" and ἱκανός • hikanós "sufficient, enough" (compare authicanopoesy "complacency"). In scholastic disputation the word was soon applied ironically to arguments which assume their own conclusions. Personal senses are seen from the seventeenth century, the pejorative "smug" chiefly from the nineteenth; standalone texts and mechanisms are so described from the late nineteenth century.
