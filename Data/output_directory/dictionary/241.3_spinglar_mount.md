241.3: spinglar "mount"

spinglar /spɪnˈglar/ [spɪŋˈglɐː]
- pin, fasten, affix, to attach with a pin or clip;
- mount, to fix (e.g. a specimen or artwork) in place for display;
- pin on, to ceremonially decorate with (a badge, etc);
- pin, pin down, restrain, hold down, to bodily prevent from moving or getting up from the ground;
- collar, nab, nail, arrest, to catch in the course of or the escape from a crime;
- pinpoint, place, identify, to realise the precise identity of

Etymology: Middle Borlish, verbal derivation from spingr "pin". This comes via synonymous Old Borlish ispingle and Vulgar Latin spīngula from a conflation of Latin spīnula "small thorn" and spīcula "small spike". Immobilisation senses not involving literal pins or similar objects arose in the sixteenth century, with the criminal sense coming from underworld argot soon afterward.
