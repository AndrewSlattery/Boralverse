238.2: outrvolar "overshoot"

outrvolar /ˌutr̩.voˈlar/ [ˌu.tɐ.vʊˈlɐː]
- (in sports) to go out of play or otherwise cross the bounds of the area of play;
- overshoot, to be fired or thrown past an intended mark or a landing place;
- (by extension) go past, to travel or end up too far;
- work loose, to slip from a hold or a fastening;
- run over, to exceed an allowable limit

Etymology: eighteenth-century coinage used in racket sports and soon thereafter hunting, a compound of outr- "beyond" and volar "fly", which both descend directly from Latin etyma ultra "beyond" and volō "I fly". More general use for overshooting a metaphorical target is seen from the mid-nineteenth century, and examples describing loose buttons or pieces of mechanisms are attested from around the same time.
