240.4: laðr "plough"

laðr /laðr̩/ [ˈla.ðɐ]
- plough, an implement drawn through the ground to break and turn it before sowing;
- blade, a curved plate fitted to a vehicle for shifting e.g. snow or rubble;
- (historical) ploughland, carucate, as much land as a single team could work in a season (reckoned for tax purposes);
- (archaic) team, outfit, workforce, crew, gang, gear and labourers needed to perform some amount of work (originally farmwork);
- grind, slog, labour that is heavy and without interesting interruption

Etymology: from Old Borlish laðre "plough" (also raðre, alaðre, arðre), various dissimulatory forms all descending from Latin arātrum "plough", a tool derivative from arō "I plough". The word is cognate with Welsh erydren and (disputedly) Merch rother, both meaning "oar". Use for groups of workers is seen from the fourteenth century and through the centralisation of farmland into the nineteenth, but fades from use with increasing mechanisation of agriculture; the final senses derive from the work-party use but have survived to the present.
