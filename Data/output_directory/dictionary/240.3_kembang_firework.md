240.3: kembang "firework"

kembang /kɛmˈbang/ [kɪmˈbaŋg]
- firework, a charge which burns or bursts with coloured light and noise;
- flare, a light fired to signal distress or to show ground at night;
- rosette, an ornament or imititation of a rose or other flower used for instance as a badge of honour;
- a shower of sparks as thrown from a forge or from a braking wheel;
- (figuratively) flash in the pan, one-hit wonder, nine days' wonder, a showy but brief success or fame

Etymology: sixteenth century, via Welsh and other European and N African accounts of the Great Borunesk [Borneo] Meeting of 1517. A borrowing of Bāsan [Indonesian] kembang "flower", presumably clipped from the compound kembangapi "flower-fire", itself a calque of hanabi in Nivoner [Japanese]. The final sense appears in an isolated use in the seventeeth-century epic poem Dottr d'Athen "Daughters of Athens", and regularly since the 1970s.
