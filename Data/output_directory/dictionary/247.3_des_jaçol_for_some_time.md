247.3: des jaçol "for some time"

des jaçol /dɛz ʒaˈtsɔl/ [dɛz ʝɐˈtsɔl]
- for some time, for ages, since some point in the past and still now;
- of old, since time immemorial, (of a custom or a belief) for so long as to need no justification;
- (interjection) about time, at last, finally, an expression of exasperation that something has taken so long

Etymology: Middle Borlish phrase of des "out of" < Latin dē ex and jaçol "long ago", an Old Borlish adverb of uncertain origin (perhaps ja de sol "already sun"?). The second sense comes from early modern legal usage, and the interjection is a twentieth-century innovation.
