243.2: noceu "toxic"

noceu /noˈtsau/ [nʊˈtsau]
- noxious, harmful, toxic, injurious to health when inhaled or swallowed;
- spoiled, rotten, gone off, unfit to eat;
- vicious, savage, liable to bite or kick;
- hateful, spiteful, antipathetic, preventing friendly relations by antagonistic behaviour;
- (law) injurious, tortious, causing harm for which damages may be claimed;
- (of weather or a season) unwholesome, sickly, bringing illness through e.g. damp or miasma

Etymology: directly from Old Borlish nociu "harmful, hurtful", from Latin nocīvus "harmful, injurious", an adjectival derivation of verb noceō "I harm, I hurt" (compare cateu < captīvus for the development). The word was used in law from early on, at first of animals and by the fourteenth century of any actionable harm, after the use of noxius in legal Latin. Application to spoiled food is Middle Borlish; that to weather and seasons, from the early modern period, reflects contemporary medical notions of unwholesome airs. The interpersonal sense is from the twentieth century.
