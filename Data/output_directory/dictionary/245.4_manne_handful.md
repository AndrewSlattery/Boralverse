245.4: manne "handful"

manne /maˈne/ [mɐˈne]
- handful, fistful, as much as one hand can hold or grasp;
- hand, the cards dealt to a single player;
- round, deal, a single dealing of the cards and its play (also in extended use to other board games);
- a few, a scattering, a number that could be counted on one hand;
- lot, fortune, the circumstances one is dealt and must make the best of

Etymology: Middle Borlish, from man "hand" (from Latin manus) and -e, a highly productive suffix of quantity or measure whose provenance is uncertain: it may be borrowed from Old French -é (from Latin -ātus), or else continue Latin -ēdō (compare e.g. forcotte "forkful", sdroyette "USB-ful"). The card-playing senses are seen from the seventeenth century and the figurative "lot, fortune" from the eighteenth; extension of the sense "round" to board games is from the twentieth.
