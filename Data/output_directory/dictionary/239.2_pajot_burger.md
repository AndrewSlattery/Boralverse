239.2: pajot "burger"

pajot /paˈʒɔt/ [pɐˈʝɔt]
- patty, burger, hamburger, a round of minced meat pressed flat and cooked (or a sandwich with this as a filling);
- cake, bar, a flat round mass of any substance pressed or moulded to shape (as of e.g. soap);
- (obsolete or historical) poultice, compress, a soft mass bound in cloth and pressed against a wound or other bodily pain;
- (military slang) mine, landmine, a flat cylindrical explosive charge laid on or just below the ground;

Etymology: Middle Borlish, via Norman from Doccian [Occitan] pallota "patty, squat cylinder of food", apparently a diminutive formation from palla "straw, animal fodder". This seems to have been influenced semantically by unrelated pala "blade, paddle, shovel" (perhaps due to a dialectal byform pall, which may reflect a variant palium in Vulgar Latin). The military sense arose during the City Wars of the 1940s and 50s; the medical sense was partially revived in the 1960s by writers of historical fiction.
