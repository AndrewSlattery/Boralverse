244.2: amo-tamo "bisexual"

amo-tamo /aˌmo.taˈmo/ [ɐˌmo.tɐˈmo]
- bisexual, bi, attracted to men and women alike (also tamo);
- catholic, unfussy, omnivorous, open-minded, liking both or all options rather than choosing between them (e.g. of food or music);
- having it both ways, enjoying the advantages of two positions usually thought incompatible;
- (advertising) two-in-one, dual-purpose, convertible, serving two functions at once (e.g. a sofa bed, a shampoo-conditioner)

Etymology: current in the nineteenth century among the tovaric communities of the Riverine Cities, from a Slavone idiom meaning "back and forth, to and fro", most likely Crovatian amo-tamo. The word is attested in Romance languages some decades later, perhaps reinforced by a folk-etymology linking it with Tuscan tutt'amo "I love all". It reaches Borlish in the 1910s, at first only in the sense "bisexual"; the extended senses developed over the twentieth century, and the clipping tamo is seen only from the 1970s.
