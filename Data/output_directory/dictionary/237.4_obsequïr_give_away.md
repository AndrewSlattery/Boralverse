237.4: obsequïr "give away"

obsequïr /ɔbˌze.kwajˈɪr/ [ʊbˌze.kwɐˈʝɪː]
- follow, defer to, comply with, to fall in with another's wishes without demur;
- indulge, humour, gratify, to give someone what they want in order to please them;
- make over, cede, give up, to part with something in another's favour;
- give away, to present a bride at her wedding

Etymology: legal term borrowed in the fifteenth century from Latin obsequor "I comply with; I indulge in". The latter senses appear to have developed by association with related obsequy "funeral ceremony, reading of a will", a now-obsolete noun from Latin obsequiae "funeral rites", and grew out of the archaic sense "bequeath". Some avoid the term in serious use for the last sense, which may sound chauvinistic or else unintentionally comical.
