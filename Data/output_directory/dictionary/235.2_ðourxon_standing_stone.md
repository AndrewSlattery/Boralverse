235.2: ðourxon "standing stone"

ðourxon /ðurˈksɔn/ [ðʊːˈksɔn]
- menhir, standing stone, megalith, orthostat, talayot, a tall upright stone acting a monument and erected in ancient times;
- (with plural agreement) stone circle, henge, cromlech, dolmen, an ancient structure constructed of standing stones used as a tomb or for other ritual purposes;
- (mythology, fantasy) giant, titan, jotun, a member of any race of superhumanly tall humanoid beings

Etymology: from northern dialect ðourskan "menhir", allied with Scots Guile [Scottish Gaelic] tursaghan "stone circle". These both originate in Old Norse þurs "ogre, monster"; standing stones across northwestern Europe were described as the petrified forms of legendary giants. The term entered standard Borlish in the seventeenth century, and has been used for legendary creatures in imitation of its Norse etymon since that was uncovered in the nineteenth century.
