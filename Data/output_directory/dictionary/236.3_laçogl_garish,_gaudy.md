236.3: laçogl "garish, gaudy"

laçogl /laˈtsɔjl/ [lɐˈtsɔil]
- eye-catching, striking, arresting, attracting the gaze at once;
- gaudy, garish, loud, bright to the point of vulgarity;
- ostentatious, flashy, dressed so as to be noticed;
- conspicuous, made deliberately to draw attention

Etymology: early modern Borlish, an exocentric compound of laçar "catch" and ogl "eye". The verb laçar was borrowed in the Old Borlish period from Old English læċċan "grab, seize, apprehend", while the noun ogl descends directly from synoymous oculus in Latin. In practice the term usually has derisive connotations of either classlessness or deceit.
