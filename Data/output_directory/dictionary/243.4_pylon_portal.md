243.4: pylon "portal"

pylon /piˈlɔn/ [pɪˈlɔn]
- gateway, propylaeum, a monumental entrance of e.g. a temple or palace flanked by towers;
- gatehouse, lodge, the building at the entrance to an estate or a walled town;
- (parachthon) portal, wormhole, a magical or technological doorway or passage leading to a distant location or another time period;
- (by extension) teleporter, time machine, a device capable of transporting cargo or passengers discontinuously across space or time

Etymology: an early modern borrowing via Latin from Greek πυλών • pulṓn "gateway, gatehouse", from πύλη • púlē "gate". Originally a term of antiquarian writing for the towered gateways of Egyptian temples, the word was extended to other entrance structures by the eighteenth century; outside historical contexts both senses are now somewhat formal. The parachthon sense arose in the Futurism period of the late nineteenth century, and was extended to devices of any shape over the twentieth.
