236.4: immoðir "bog down"

immoðir /ˌɪ.moˈðɪr/ [ˌɪ.mʊˈðɪː]
- wallow, to settle or sink slowly into mud;
- mire, bog down, to cause to be stuck in mud or soft ground;
- daub, render, to coat with mud or clay (as of walls);
- patch up, whitewash, gloss over, to conceal a fault hurriedly from view

Etymology: Middle Borlish, inchoative verbal derivation from moð "mud, muck". This noun, a variant of now-obsolete moðr, was borrowed from a descendant (possibly Frise) of Classical German muthras "excrement, filth, sediment". Metaphorical extension of the building-material sense to hiding (but not correcting) faults is seen from as early as the fifteenth century.
