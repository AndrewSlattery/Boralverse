245.2: mandovy "peanut"

mandovy /ˌman.doˈvi/ [ˌman.dʊˈvi]
- peanut, groundnut, the plant or its edible seed which ripens underground in a papery pod;
- peanut butter, the ground nut used as a spread or a dressing;
- nutshell, a brief summary containing the essential gist of a matter;
- (slang, plural only) stash, nest egg, savings or valuables hidden e.g. around the house or in a separate account

Etymology: originally in the staddenzen Borlish of the Novomund, a borrowing from Yengatow [Tupi] mandubi "peanut". The sense "peanut butter" is a twentieth-century clipping of saus de mandovy "peanut sauce". The sense "nutshell" arises in the nineteenth century as an alteration of earlier ne nouç "in a walnut", itself after a Classical anecdote due to Pliny of an Iliad copied small enough to fit in a nutshell. The slang sense, from the mid-twentieth century, probably alludes to the plant burying its own pods.
