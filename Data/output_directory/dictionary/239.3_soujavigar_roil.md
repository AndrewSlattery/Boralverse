239.3: soujavigar "roil"

soujavigar /suˌʒa.viˈgar/ [sʊˌʝa.vɪˈgɐː]
- dredge, to deepen a channel or a harbour by scooping out its bed;
- drag, to search the bed of a water for something lost;
- roil, agitate, to stir up settled silt and so cloud the water;
- rabblerouse, demagogue, to make angry or riotous using inflammatory speech

Etymology: sixteenth century, compound of sou- "under" (from Latin prefix sub- "under") and javigar "trawl; scour, comb through". The verb derives from noun javig "trawlnet", borrowed in the thirteenth century from synonymous xaviga in Portingale. This in turn reflects Arabic شبكة • shabaca "fishing net". In a political context the term is seen from accounts of the lead-up to the Democratic Wars of the eighteenth century.
