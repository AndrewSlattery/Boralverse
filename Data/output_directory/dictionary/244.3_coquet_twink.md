244.3: coquet "twink"

coquet /koˈkwɛt/ [kʊˈkwɛt]
- twink, a slender, smooth-skinned young man considered as an object of desire;
- pretty boy, fop, peacock, a vain young man more concerned with his looks than with substance;
- (dated) bantam, strutter, a cocky youth who preens and swaggers;
- fresh face, ingénue, new arrival, a young newcomer to a scene or a profession;
- (technology) lite, slimline, mini, a pared-down and lightweight model of e.g. a phone or a bicycle

Etymology: a twentieth-century merger of two words. The first is a native diminutive of coq "cock, rooster", used in Middle Borlish of cockerels and from the sixteenth century of cocky youths, which survives in the dated sense. The second is Furlain coquetta "dolly, poppet", a diminutive of Zahid Russian cocla "doll, puppet", which came via Malchassian Greek κούκλα • koúkla "doll" from Latin cuculla, cucullus "cowl, hood". The Furlain word reached Borlish in the early twentieth century through tovaric slang, and supplies the senses of youthful prettiness; the sense of a newcomer is from mid-century theatre slang, and the technological sense from advertising of the 1980s.
