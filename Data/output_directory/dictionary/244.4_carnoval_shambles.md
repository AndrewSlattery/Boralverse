244.4: carnoval "shambles"

carnoval /ˌkar.noˈval/ [ˌkɐː.nʊˈval]
- Carnival, Shrovetide, Mardi Gras, the days of feasting and licence before Lent;
- send-off, last hurrah, a final indulgence before an abstinence or a hardship begins;
- misrule, topsy-turvydom, a set period in which the usual order is suspended or inverted;
- masquerade, a street procession or private event where people are masked or costumed;
- (figuratively) farce, shambles, proceedings which have lost all dignity

Etymology: a fifteenth-century borrowing from Tuscan carnovale, a variant of carnevale contracted from Old Tuscan carnelevare "Shrovetide", literally "removal of meat", from Latin carō (stem carn-) "meat" and levāre "lift, remove" (whence also native leyar "raise, lift"). At first used of the festivities of Venis and Rom, it was applied to Borland's own Shrovetide customs by the seventeenth century; the learned folk etymology carne vale "farewell, meat!" is current from about the same time. The figurative senses are seen from the eighteenth century, the sense "farce" chiefly in political journalism of the nineteenth.
