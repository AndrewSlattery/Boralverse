245.3: passar roug "struggle"

passar roug /paˈsar ruj/ [pɐˈsɐː ʀuj]
- struggle, flounder, have a hard time of it, to meet one setback after another;
- run at a loss, be in deficit, to spend more than comes in;
- (medicine) ail, worsen, to take a turn for the worse;
- (education) fail, be marked down, to come back covered in corrections

Etymology: idiom first attested in seventeenth-century merchants' correspondence, literally "to spend (time) red", from the practice of entering debts and losses in red ink in ledgers. The medical sense, from the eighteenth century, was likely helped along by the flush of fever, and the scholastic sense a century later by teachers' red-ink corrections. The general sense of struggling is from the twentieth century.

Y varous pas roug cay tre annað.
/i vaˈruz paz ruj ke tre aˈnaθ/
[i vɐˈʀuz paz ʀuj ke tʀe ɐˈnah]
df shop pass red fall three year
The shop has lost money for three years.
