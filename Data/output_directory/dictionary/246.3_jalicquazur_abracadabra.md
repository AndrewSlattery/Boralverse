246.3: jalicquazur "abracadabra"

jalicquazur /ˌʒa.li.kwaˈzɪr/ [ˌʝa.lɪ.kwɐˈzɪː]
- (interjection) hey presto, abracadabra, alakazam, the word spoken at the climax of a conjuring trick (also in metaphorical use);
- sleight of hand, legerdemain, conjuring, stage magic or a single magic trick;
- hocus pocus, a claimed marvel or relic dismissed as stage trickery;
- flimflam, sharp practice, a swindle worked by misdirection;
- jiggery-pokery, fiddling, fudging the numbers, cooking the books, underhand manipulation of accounts or results

Etymology: late eighteenth century, from y Jalicq Azur "the Bluejacket" (from jalicq "waistcoat, tuxedo", ultimately from Turk yelec via Arabic and Portingale, and azur "blue", via Portingale from Arabic), the stage name of an illusionist who played to Damvath and Cordin society during the Long Peace. The epenthetic /-w-/ reflects an older pronunciation of q-final words before vowels.

Though still unidentified, it has been suggested that the Bluejacket was a woman in men's clothing, possibly part of the nascent Deviance movement. The name appears to have been shouted by the audience at the climaxes of Bluejacket's illusions. The dismissive sense flourished in the polemics surrounding the Laic Declarations, and the financial senses are from the twentieth century.
