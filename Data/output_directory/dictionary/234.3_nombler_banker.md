234.3: nombler "banker"

nombler /nɔmˈblɛr/ [nʊmˈblɛː]
- (historical) moneychanger, one who exchanges coin of different mints for a fee;
- banker, financier, one whose business is the keeping and lending of money;
- (games) banker, dealer, the house, the player who holds the stakes and pays out winnings;
- a type of hand-cake incorporating jam and iced with marzipan

Etymology: descendant of Old Borlish nomblair "money-changer" either directly from synonymous nummulārius in Latin or via nomble "coinage, small-value coins". Both of these come from Latin nummulus "a small amount of money", diminutive of nummus "coin". The term has usually referred to more institutionalised positions since the sixteenth century, when it also gained its gambling sense. In the last sense the term is a calque of Angelican Welsh tessen nawiddwr "banker's cake", an invented tradition of the late nineteenth century spurred by the boom in almond farming in the region.
