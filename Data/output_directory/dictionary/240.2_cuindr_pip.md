240.2: cuindr "pip"

cuindr /kaindr̩/ [ˈkain.dɐ]
- pip, stone, a hard seed inside a fruit;
- gland, node, a small firm swelling in the flesh under the skin;
- knot, gristle, a hard lump in meat or muscle;
- grudge, grievance, resentment, the negative feelings associated with a past injustice (real or imagined)

Etymology: directly from Old Borlish curnle, cuinle "pip, fruit stone; grain (of cereal)". This was borrowed from Old English cyrnel "seed, acorn, kernel", a diminutive of the ancestor of "corn". The senses relating to meat and flesh appear to be present in the Old English, but may be an independent development in early modern Borlish; the emotional use is seen from as early as the sixteenth century.
