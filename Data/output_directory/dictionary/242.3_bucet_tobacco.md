242.3: bucet "tobacco"

bucet /biˈtsɛt/ [bɪˈtsɛt]
- tobacco, the plant or its cured leaves prepared for e.g. smoking or chewing;
- smoke break, a short pause from work especially as taken to smoke;
- (dated) tip, gratuity, pourboire, lagniappe, small change given for a service (as if to buy tobacco with);
- (in apposition) tobacco-brown, snuff-coloured, of a dull yellowish brown

Etymology: sixteenth century, via Vascon or Portingale buchete and Arabic بشيت • bushait from contemporary Nawat [Classical Nahuatl] pisietl "tobacco plant". In the sense of "tip", seen primarily in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Compare butchet (also butch) in Merch, 寶屑 • pǒushet in southern (imperial) Chinese. Use as a colour term tends to connote dinginess or an ill-maintained quality.
