239.5: Events of 1824

notes on various events which occurred in 1824 (recalling that the Nuntial calendar year runs from March to February).

The Majoric [Hungarian] canon Berzéthy Antal claimed on 1824 April 3 to have deciphered the Mysarnos Codex, using a series of context-dependent subsitution rules mapping the glyphs to Latin letters. Berzéthy liberally re-interpreted the resulting text as a collection of devotional psalms. The Library of Sobjan [Pécs] issued him with a commemorative medal and an increased stipend.

At the Verona Arena (the Roman amphitheatre having been recently restored in the aftermath of the Second German War), Chiarina Bruno's opera Ale in Fiamme was first performed on 1824 June 12. The composition incorporated off-stage string instruments tuned flat to depict the viol cemtir (death-spirits of Borlish folklore), an element of stories related her twin brother Jacomo told her. Jacomo had served with Borlish soldiers at the 1821 Battle of Hulm [Ulm].

Some months later, on 1824 October 5 a fraudulent transmission by the flag-lamp steeplepost [opticial telegraph] in Ambrosia led the mechanical display in Aiga d'Angoixa [Quebec City] to mistakenly announce the death of governor Guillem Arrosteguy. This was followed on October 11 by a false declaration of war with Ambrosia's southern neighbour New Provence. The resulting panic famously allowed broker Amadeu Lascorz to become (briefly, before his arrest) the richest person in Ambrosia before ships arrived from Gorram [a city in New Jersey] on October 12 with the truth.

The Vascon farm theorist Ricardo Ivarrachezuri presented research on 1824 November 20 documenting that semen from male animals rapidly becomes non-viable if stored in copper syringes; he proposed that this was by the same mechanism by which copper vessels kill zoia [micro-organisms] and prevent infections.

On 1824 January 24 the Borlish mathematician Darren Brodus published a refutation of the Berzéthy decipherment in fewer than 400 words. By applying Berzéthy’s substitution rules to items from the previous week's Axbane shipping register, Brodus generated devotional prose which was just as coherent as Berzéthy's translations.

The following Monday, the assayer and alchemist Yorreth ap Trahaen isolated an unknown mineral (mẃn kildarec "killdark ore") from the mines at Killdara [Kildare] in Ireland. The material was initially monopolised by a Dublin pottery maker to create yellow-green glazes.
