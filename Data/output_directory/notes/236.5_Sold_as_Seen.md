236.5: Sold as Seen

reproduced excerpt from the auction catalogue selling off the estate of Perrina Zancoud, last of a wealthy Santrafew [~Boston] family descended from Reverend Carol Zancoud as well as the New Provincial cadet branch of the Nassow dynasty.
The annotations are from 2021 paper Collection and Separation of Nappe [Lenape] Artefacts in and from Eastern Mendeva, 1780–1930, written by domain historian Ilvana Grasset, department chair at the University of Gorram.

…each lot is sold as seen [1]. Ladies and gentlemen are reminded that the packet runs late in the next days to ferry buyers to Campottonì [Wilmington, DE] from Gorram.

HANGINGS, DRAPES and TAPESTRIES (three hundred feet) in the Morrack fashion. From 12 trone.

SEAL-CARVING of DEREK NASSOW, silver, bearing the sigil of the Avosche duchy. Brought to New Provence in 1590 by the duke himself [2], and a relic of an unexplained flight across the Atlantic [2a]. From 400 trone.

GIRDLE, worked leather and shell, of Mastoisèt [~Massachusett] make [3]. From 6 trone.

WITHDRAWN.

THREE VOLUMES of the DIARY of and LETTERS to a woman of Santrafew [4], from the years 1589–94 and 1601–3, with some water damage throughout. From 25 trone.

ACCOUNT BOOK of the fur trade via the Becouin and Esquanna rivers, in three hands, amounting to eleven thousand…

===

[1] A term renouncing any claims as to provenance or true value. This auction-house (Rocabrun e Fills) uses the designation over twice as often as other establishments in 1890s New Provence.

[2, 2a] Derek Nassow was never duke. Grandson of the duchess Adaillé d'Avosche, the fact that he left his family permanently after an uninformative public announcement in 1589 (south to Provence and then to the Novomund [New World]) has been explained variously in subsequent centuries as due to debts, mistresses, a duel or a religious conversion.

[3] Probably, one of the Mastoisèt records of the Becouin Treaty of 1621, a compact broken by the Provincial settlers after the British sack of Santrafew. It sold for 7 trone to tradesman Casimir Vergonn, of Marsella, and is presumed lost in the 1907 bombing of that city.

[4] The writings by and to Nadàlla fi Carol are the most detailed record we have of the early years of Provincial settlement on the east coast of Mendeva; this lot comprises volumes 2, 3 and 6 of a set that has never been completely recovered. The fourth volume surfaced in Ambrosia in 1931 and is currently in the New Vithor University's public collection on Borland.
