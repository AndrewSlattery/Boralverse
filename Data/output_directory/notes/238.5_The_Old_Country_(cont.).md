238.5: The Old Country (cont.)

continued excerpt from The Old Country, a tourist guidebook about Britain aimed at travellers from British Mendeva written by Ossewma'h Ariandro.

…the other controlling Wales, and so Wales was permitted to hold itself. In 1121 the arrangement nearly collapsed—Vigo VII, king of Devon, launched a failed invasion of the Tremonnow [Monmouth] region without the Emperor's blessing (Roger II was in Paris with a depleted treasury and a Flemish impasse).

Alan VI split Devon from Kernow in the realignment of 1225, some decades after the disintegration of the Drengot Empire, and Wales spent the rest of the century collecting counties. In 1280 the lord Ivan of Cartaw [Cardiff] conquered what remained of Devon of (it seems) his own accord—his king, David, showed some rare good sense and decided he had authorised the act all along. Thus he declared the Tallath of Devon and granted it to Ivan's son Marduth, Ivan having died of a fever before he could enjoy his spoils.
