236.1: A Hand of Arrows

A scene in translation from Act II of the 1921 social comedy A Hand of Arrows, written by Fayencetta Deft, which was first staged at the Upgate Theatre in London. 

(Sur y terraçment a Majon Limeric. Scein son lontan e scompiglous de martelment e de baðr ottonam. VERATRA s'appen cos periglous doutr y cancel dy porrujon.)
(The terrace of Limerick House. The distant, chaotic sound of hammering and brass instruments. VERATRA leans dangerously far over the balcony railing.)

DOMN CHALFONT: Y torn te vien por ençar, Veratra. Preðauc tu vel pretendr exeunt fair par diverjon.
LADY CHALFONT: It is your turn to lead, Veratra. Unless you intend to forfeit by way of distraction.

VERATRA: Ðe son attenent a y gamblas de bourgmer hormejar, ma y colanç tien a l'oncar vars y najel soujavigour. L'oc es voutent cos tars. Y musicour pariscn toð sfreyað.
VERATRA: They’re trying to moor the mayor’s barge, but the current keeps dragging it toward the dredging boats. It’s absolutely entrancing. The musicians look terrified.

DOMN CHALFONT: (Orn dant ag manne) Ðe son s'immoðiscent paulatim des aubon. Fig ty blad. Jo say l'ig tu es saget bacagettant.
LADY CHALFONT: (Sorting her hand) They have been sinking into the mud since dawn. Play your card. I know you are hoarding arrows.

VERATRA: (Se tornant vars y tabr) Soul jo stim l'ensign me plag cas heu. (La fig un blad) Seg de saget.
VERATRA: (Turning to the table) I merely find the suit aesthetically pleasing. (She plays a card) Sice of arrows.

DOMN CHALFONT: Un ovartur lon audaç. (La fig un blad) Licol de gem.
LADY CHALFONT: A timid opening. (She plays a card) Deuce of gems.

VERATRA: Tu fay tojorn partejan reyal, Morrig.
VERATRA: You always were a royalist, Aunt.

(Un porter, FISH, entr ac cargað d'un per de cocheyau carnoval a pegnt laçogl eð abreu d'aug.)
(A footman, FISH, enters carrying a pair of waterlogged, brightly painted carnival stilts.)
