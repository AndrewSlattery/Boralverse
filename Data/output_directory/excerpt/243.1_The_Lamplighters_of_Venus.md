243.1: The Lamplighters of Venus

excerpt in translation from The Lamplighters of Venus, an 1808 trevold written by Kentish author Rosamund Petifer in evident imitation of Elsebeth Sneider's Through a Black Mirror.

Jo sta sur y pont con y capitan candon y preu lampion se mettoirn a vijon noscon dessou, cluinant ne ranc lonc y rimot com y campaner cas nos, e le jurau ðe dreçan un bouð.
I was on deck with the captain when the first lamps showed below us, winking in long rows along the coast like the steepleposts of home, and he swore they were spelling out a message.

Alcun hom abord poðe lo leir, ma Sr Aske declarau lo sta indoutabr un xeny, dec necun pouvr sur y Tar ben allors vel tan sforç fair for a tug d'impoliðeç.
Nobody aboard could read it, but Mr Aske declared it was certainly a welcome, since no people on Earth or off it would go to so much trouble merely to be rude.

Y gent dy havan, eç remeyant avars por nos encontrar, no stan hautessem a figl, e stan robað ne vert des pegl vars caucet.
The harbour-folk who rowed out to meet us were no taller than children and dressed in green from cap to stocking.

Lorry parol ern toð hoissað, ma lorry ustolment sta civil aðief por ig, pre un hour, nos fom ðescað, pounçað eð effurað dy mitað de nossy bucet.
Their speech was all whistles, yet their manners were so very civil that within the hour we had been fed, flattered and relieved of half our butchet [tobacco].

Jo lo nommau runam com rouvaç, con tan, jo l'admet, lo fos y rouvaç ajoutandessem meyon jamay sbolognað.
I privately called it robbery, though I confess it was the pleasantest robbery I have ever suffered.
