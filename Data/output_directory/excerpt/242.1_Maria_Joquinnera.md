242.1: Maria Joquinnera

notes in translation on a sixteenth-century figure.

Maria Joquinnera, tan dignant y Princes de Mendeva, sta un domn d'orin Nappesc, nað lonc y Guittoc bassem, e la veyaçau ag Vetomont vars y fin ag dezein 1530.
Maria Joquinnera, also known as the Princess of Mendeva, was a woman of Nappe [Lenape] origin, born by the lower Guittoc [Delaware] River, who travelled to the Vetomund [Old World] in the late 1530s.

Ostendað ne plusour cort Vetomondin com y princes desracinað d'un gent Mendevan, la sta, lonc toð y tesmogn temporane, un glossomath e un çavel politic excellent.
Presented at several Vetomundane courts as the displaced princess of a Mendevan people, she was by all contemporary accounts a formidable polyglot and political intellect.

La parola deja Docçan candon l'alandau a Marsegl, e la parsisceu cos raut plusour autr lengaç European.
She already spoke Doccian [~Occitan] when she arrived in Marsella, and soon picked up several other European tongues.

Sy seucq pardurandessem interman un onomatoir dy lengaç Nappesc; ny rup la's nommað com patron eð addoçour principal y dou.
Her most enduring legacy is a dictionary of the Nappe tongue, in whose front matter she is named as both patron and primary informant.

L'enteressem entr y codex sceint contien atorn 1400 parol, cosogr locuçon fast, claus de traitanç e un scrit brey, oc stant parcanç un cançon de cordougl ben de xeny solemn.
The most complete surviving manuscript contains some 1400 words, along with set phrases, diplomatic formulae and a short text which may be a song of mourning or of formal welcome.

Sell'heir Barthem parola ancour Nappesc tras sy quoncein, ma con un son Docçan oç, lonc y regest, ayent sy maðr amusað auvorn.
Her son Barthem spoke Nappe into adulthood, albeit with a Doccian accent that is recorded as having greatly amused his mother.
