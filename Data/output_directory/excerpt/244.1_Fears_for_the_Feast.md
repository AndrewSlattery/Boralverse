244.1: Fears for the Feast

letter and reply in translation from the advice column of Niacer, a Borlish Sunday magazine, in its issue of 1974 December 15. The column employs an anonymous team of counsellors labelled only by number.

My bel maðr es y tort dell'ivan torrent por y Fest Reveglon de nossy parroiç cay may a trent annað.
My mother-in-law has baked the child's pie for our parish's Winter Feast for over thirty years.

Dec sy man son affauðant, l'a me peðið a lo torrir poll'oc Reveglon.
Since her hands are failing her she has asked me to bake it for this Revillon.

Ma la flagit ig jo obsequïsc sy method, oc meyon tojorn trovað un pog sec, eð ig jo benoç y croç boscer parey seyon tojorn covauclið ne sy tort.
But she insists that I follow her recipe, which I've always found rather dry, and that I use the same wooden cross that she's always hidden in her pies.

Jo vel magn faïr y tort fair a my façon meðes.
I would much prefer to make the pie my own way.

Me stou veram fair com la dig? — Sr Covart-de-Farin
Must I really do as she says? — Mr Covered-in-Flour

n7: Torrað lo a sy façon, for meðes poll'oc annað.
n7: Bake it her way, if only for this year.

La vos tramet un plaç ag caf de sy tabr de trent annað, e la desir lo veir ne bon man.
She's handing you a place at the head of her table of thirty years, and she wants to see it in good hands.

Alcun hom ny Fest jamira ða y past es sec.
Nobody at the Feast will mind if the pastry is dry.

Vos arreð plausibr y Reveglon sevent por vossy tort meðes.
You will likely have next Revillon for your own pie.

Costojeð y croç.
Keep the cross.
