246.1: Stobor

excerpt in translation from Figures of Fable, a 2004 compendium of fantastical beings from the world's folklore, written by Gorram folklorist Florença Garaut.

Stobor es un claus magn abrangent por toð entað eç smargent des depegnt artifiçal de gent (ben d'animal, ou sceint ben parachthon) aðelið con anim par thaumat, ben eç aglon pariscent com figurin ben haval veunt.
Stobor is a broad term for any beings which arise from the magical animation of artificial depictions of people (or animals, or nonexistent creatures), or which otherwise take the form of animated figurines or dolls.

Lonc y costumn, stobor son haval, ma n'ascreuçon recendessem y claus es slargið vars figur de tagl tesqual com imascry veunt, ben meðes, parveg, colos maissem ag við.
Traditionally stobor are miniatures, but in more recent taxonomy the term has been broadened to life-size figures like animated statues, or even, occasionally, larger-than-life colossi.

Y parol stobor fey all'origin sprit connoscr ny cont a pouvr d'Avry e de Kieu, eç haval d'ivan aðelent con anim, lorry aventur minuscr souvant por lou meðes ben gest sarvent ben cateu fligtant par y majon.
The word stobor first referred to sprites in the folklore of Awry [~Hungary] and Kiew [~Ukraine] which animated children's dolls, going on miniature adventures of their own or performing helpful or mischievous deeds around the house.

Gent tras y mont figurn des jaçol lorry depegnt seyon forgað a un við autonom broucar.
Peoples the world over have imagined the images they create taking on a life of their own.

Certan dy civiltað veðerrem forgaurn figurin com y voscapty Egypçan ben y soldart de Lleswan por sona luïr de pedisecq ben de costojan ny við dell'outrmort.
Some of the oldest civilisations made figurines like the Egyptian voshapty [ushabti] or the warriors of Lleswan [Terracotta Army] to act as attendants or guardians in the afterlife.
