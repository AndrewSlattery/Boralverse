241.1: Jeans

notes in translation on the early history of Jeans.

Y nom Jen fay un polity Italian eð un citað dignant parey connoscr.
Jeans refers to an Italian polity and to a city of the same name.

Lo's abrangent dy rimmot Miterran ag norvest d'Italy eð ag tarmn dy Provenç, tan com l'istr de Corsc.
It comprises the northwest Middlesea coastline of Italy bordering Provence, as well as the island of Corse.

Jen es y veðerrem entr y Republic Latin, ayent se mis sur y mont ny siecr deg.
Jeans is the oldest of the Latin Republics, having come into existence in the tenth century.

Cosogr Venis, l'interman un pis contr y noçon ig l'epoc de Scon Tetrarchy nell'eç siecr onç e doç sta cos soul un panelleyaçon de gran empir fort unfeyað.
Along with Venise, it serves as a counterpoint to the notion that the Second Tetrarchy period in the eleventh and twelfth centuries was solely composed of great centralised empires.

Y directour Jennesc, connuð sou nom de console, er sleut des entrig dell'aristocrat marcander.
The leader of Jeans, known as the console, was elected from among the merchant nobility.

D'an 1207N Jen accomette l'envajon dell'Istr Tyrrhen de Corsc e Sardegn, lou cahottant de gournanç Roman.
In 1207N Jeans invaded the Tyrrhene Isles of Corse and Sardine, wresting them from Roman control.

For y norvestessem entr y dou istr vole remanir all'entarn dell'orisont Jennesc cos longinquessem.
Only the northernmost of the two islands would remain in the Jean orbit more permanently.
