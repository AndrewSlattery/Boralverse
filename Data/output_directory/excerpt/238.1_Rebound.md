238.1: Rebound

notes in translation on rebound, a wall-and-ball game of the Middlesea [Mediterranean] coast. Unlike lineball there is no unified ruleset, although some polities do stage tournment play of local variants.

Sur casc rimot dy Miterran, toð citað ben toun ec y nom meritant broucn un mur a rebont.
On every coast of the Middlesea, any city or town worthy of the name boasts a rebound wall.

Dou compleis, ben dou per den—ben a gest un nombr quidam d'alumn felau ben de veðr amig—battn a to-rutto y bal dur ne cogr, con lanç ben peyaç contr y mur ligs ne pieðr.
Two players, or two pairs—or in practice any number of schoolmates or old friends—strike the hard leather ball in turn, with throws or kicks against the smooth stone wall.

Un try finisc candon un hasmour poð noc y bal offirr pre y scon bondisc contr y paið, ben candon y bal outrvol y zon de joy (tragçað cos insolemn ne creð, ben durablessem con loson zajadau).
A round ends when an opponent cannot return the ball before the second bounce on the floor, or when the ball leaves the zone of play (marked informally in chalk, or more permanently with coloured paving).

Rebont interman un joy de ciel ovart e de cromn floisessem, e victour des Mysarnos vars Ẋr l Joas son connuð par nom a lau atorn.
Rebound is a game of open skies and looser purse-strings, and champions from Mysarnos to Ẋr l Joas are known by name for leagues around.

Certan hasardour son denuð risc par lorry triomph; bempart may attenoirn la e neyaurn, tragedy ottem ben pessem galant.
Some punters have grown rich off their successes; many more have tried to and failed, courting greater or lesser tragedies.
